# Scenes: the first playable text chat

**Status:** first slice of [Minimal Text Scene Runner](roadmap/sprints/minimal-text-scene-runner.md) (Sprint 035, Wave 5). Headless: there is no UI, streaming or async yet. It is three packages:
- `grimoire.scene` (pure): a played turn, its payload codec (`grimoire.scene-turn/1`), and what each listener heard;
- `grimoire.scenestore` (adapter): the `turns/` record family over one open `LibraryStore`;
- `grimoire.play` (adapter): `SceneRunner`, which plays one chat through [027](GENERATION.md)'s runtime.

All scenarios are synthetic and every model call is scripted.

## What it does

**Proving scenario** (`backend/tests/test_scene_proof.py`). Two chats are played, closed and opened again with a new store, authority and model runtime, as an app restart would:
- **A migrated V1 campaign.** [026](IMPORT_V1.md)'s Drowned Ledger import: Winifred is the player's character; Mara speaks in an authored voice and Seraphine in the voice derived from her imported card. Over three turns the player asks a question, whispers to Mara, pauses and resumes, redirects one of Seraphine's lines, saves a post and closes the app before any call. After the restart the post is still there and is answered. Mara's prompt holds the whisper; Seraphine's holds neither the whisper nor her own withdrawn line.
- **A world-less card chat.** [037](CARDS.md)'s Mara Vell card with its lorebook, and Anya Rook as the persona. The card's first greeting opens the chat; asking about the lighthouse triggers the card's lore in Mara's prompt. After a whisper, a second card (Tamsin Hale) is attached mid-chat. Tamsin's first prompt holds what was said since she arrived, and nothing before.
- **A scenario-only chat** (Storm over Port Vell) with no NPC: the only generation is a director reply the player asks for, and the policy allows.

**Across real restarts** (`backend/tests/test_scene_restart.py`). The same three chats, with every phase in a new Python process that ends with `os._exit` (nothing closed, as when the app is killed), one of them killed inside `generate` after its `running` take was written: each chat goes on exactly where it stopped, the killed run is reported `interrupted` and superseded, and the scenario-only chat gets a new director reply.

Each speaking NPC gets its own call, with its own voice, its own [031](CONTEXT.md) pack and only the lines it heard. Every call's attempts and token usage are stored with its take, even when the turn halted or went stale.

## A turn

`TurnRef(campaign_id, turn_id)` is `campaign:<c>#turn/<t>`, stored at `campaigns/<c>/turns/<t>.json` in the scope-record envelope ([STORAGE](STORAGE.md)). `turn_id` is `<chat_id>.<n:06d>`, numbered from 1 per chat. On a library with a device identity ([SYNC](SYNC.md)) a new turn's id also carries that device's tag, `<chat_id>.<n:06d>-<tag>` (`chatstore.device_tag`, the tag 028 gives new chat actors and 034 new items, [DEVICE_SYNC](DEVICE_SYNC.md)), recorded in the payload as `device`, so two paired devices that each play turn *n* offline write two turns with two ids, never one id with two contents (which 032 would read as one message, and 020 as one immutable record, once milestone 2 writes deliveries under it). A library never given an identity numbers turns as before. A turn keeps its id for good: turns played before `identify` are still read, rewritten under their own id, and play goes on from them with tagged turns; a turn planned before `identify` and written after it is refused `turn_changed`, and a damaged identity refuses every new turn (`device_identity`) rather than mint untagged ids. A payload without `device` (written before the tag existed) reads as untagged. Both forms fit 032's 104-character message ids: an untagged turn's chat id is at most 97 characters, as before tags, so every turn saved before them still reads; a tagged turn's is at most 88. So a chat whose id is 89 to 97 characters long keeps its history and plays on a library with no identity, but takes no new turn once its library is identified (`chat_id_too_long`). A chat holds at most 999,999 turns (`too_many_turns`).

A `TurnRecord` holds:
- `origin`: `post` (a player's post), `prompt` (the table asked the cast to go on), `opening` (a card's greeting or a scenario's opening), or `imported` (reserved; 036's imported history lives in its own `transcripts/` family);
- `post`: the player's line, who submitted it, its kind (`speech` or `action`), channel (one of 032's: `speech`, `whisper`, `telepathy`), addressees (a private channel needs at least one), **audience**, and whether it is `withheld` (an action its sender alone hears);
- `takes`: up to 16. A take is `running`, `proposed`, `kept`, `withdrawn`, `stale` or `superseded`. It records its cast, its lines, every planned call with its status and attempts, the policy's declined calls, any unscreened director lore, a redirect's direction, and who chose it;
- `paused` (and, kept only for `resume`, who paused), and `passed_by`;
- `follows`: the id of the turn it was played after (`null` for turn 1), and `set_aside`: [028's mark](#forked-chats) on a branch head that does not go on (`{"chosen": <turn id>, "by": <principal>}`, or `null`). A turn written before these fields has neither: it is placed after the turn before it of its own device, else the untagged one, else the only one, and never by a guess past that (`turn_gap`).

A turn is open until one take is kept or somebody passes it. Lines are `<turn_id>.<take>.<n>` and are `shown`, `withdrawn` or `struck`. Unknown top-level payload keys survive a rewrite; inside a known structure an unknown or malformed field is refused, never dropped.

**A turn is transcript, never world state.** A kept line is what the table chose to show. No event, belief, relation, effect or ruling is written, so nothing said here becomes canon a later rule could read.

## Who heard what

- A post's audience is 032's `transmit` receivers plus the sender. Room speech with nobody else present is heard by the speaker alone. An action that is no message at all (032's `not_directed`) is seen by everyone present. An action 032 cannot rule out as a private message (`unresolved_private`: a note, an aside under the breath, a nod) is **withheld**: its sender alone hears it, and it is in neither an NPC's window nor the table's. A message aimed at someone 032 cannot resolve is refused with 032's reason (`unknown_addressee`, `unclear_content`, `ambiguous_addressee`, ...); nothing 032 cannot read ever falls back to a public line.
- An NPC line is heard by everyone present, even when it answers a whisper. A narrator line is heard by the table (the present player characters), never by an NPC.
- **Audience is fixed when a line is spoken.** An actor attached later was in no earlier audience, so it hears nothing that came before. A character handed to the director later keeps what it heard as a player.
- Proposals, withdrawn lines and struck lines are heard by no one. A reroll never hears the take it replaces. A line with no words (`...`) is heard by no one either, and does not take a place in the window.
- **Imported history comes first.** A chat's [036](TRANSCRIPTS.md) transcript pages (a migrated V0.2.1 scene, a SillyTavern import) are its history before turn 1, read on every call and never copied into turns (`Scene.imported`, `grimoire.scene.imported_history`, read by `grimoire.play.history`). The pages say who wrote each post, never who heard it, so the runner decides that by one rule: **the actors the chat was imported with** (its roster at sequence 0: an importer starts the chat with its whole cast and never rebinds it) heard every shown post, and an actor attached later heard none of it, as with any late arrival. Only a post's chosen variant is heard.
  - A post by the player, a character, or a source's generated reply that names its speaker (V0.2.1's `assistant` with a label such as `Mara`) is heard by that cast and seen by the table.
  - Narration is the table's only, never an NPC's: a `narrator` post, and V0.2.1's narrator (the reserved `**Grimoire:**` speaker, which 026 writes as an `assistant` post with no label).
  - A `system` notice (a V0.2.1 synthetic post, a dice roll), a `hidden` post, an unchosen swipe and a post with no chosen text are nobody's.
  - A line's speaker is the cast member's current name when the post names its actor, else the source's label; a player post with neither (V0.2.1's `**You:**`) is the imported persona's, by name (`Player` if there is none), and a label with no words (`???`) falls back to the role.
  - Imported lines take places in the same window as played lines; only the last `MAX_HEARD` of each view are built.
  - If any of the chat's pages cannot be read, the numbering has a gap, or a SillyTavern import's page 1 says it wrote more pages than there are (`missing_pages`), **nobody hears any of it** (fail closed: a listener would otherwise hear part of the history, unmarked), and `open` reports `imported_history_unreadable`. A campaign `transcripts` folder that cannot be walked (a link, a file) may hide any chat's pages, so it does the same for every chat of the campaign; a look-alike sibling (`Transcripts`) does the same for a chat with pages, and is only reported (`transcripts`) for a chat with none.
- `heard_for` gives an NPC's window (the last `MAX_HEARD` lines, each capped at 027's `MAX_LINE_CHARS`); it is both its brief's `heard` and its pack's `scene_text`. `table_lines` is the director's view: the imported history the table saw, every post but a private one, and every kept line.

## The runner

`SceneRunner(store, authority, handle, *, owner, runtime=None, policy=None, secrets=(), voices=None, fence=None, budget=DEFAULT_BUDGET)`.
- `handle` is a trusted human handle `authority` issued (the library owner's). It writes turn files and records control at `seat`.
- `runtime` is the app's `GenerationRuntime`: a trusted app handle, the configured provider only, no approvals asked. Without one, nothing is generated (`no_runtime`).
- `policy` caps the calls a turn may plan; a director reply needs `director_generation`.
- `secrets` are screened in every brief. With none, only 031's director-lore markers are screened.
- `voices` are authored profiles; an imported character with none derives its voice from its card (027/026), and one with no example lines speaks anyway.
- `fence` makes an older run of the same chat stale.

Every method reads the chat afresh (one `LibraryView`, 022's chat snapshot, the turns, 010b's recorded control, 033's party) and writes at most one turn file.

| Method | What it does |
|---|---|
| `open(chat)` | The `Scene` as it stands: turns, cast, persona, names, controls and problems (a damaged turn, an `interrupted` run, a bound source that is gone: `broken_source` with its exact ref, imported pages that cannot all be read: `imported_history_unreadable` with each page's problem (or `transcripts` for the campaign's folder, in a chat with none), a cast member with `no_voice`, a party member left out (`party_uncertain`, `missing_actor`, `instance_id_conflict`, `cast_unstaged`), a persona the director controls: `persona_not_player`). Writes nothing. |
| `seat(chat, player)` | Records control for actors with none: the persona to `player`, every other bound character to the director. A recorded controller is never changed. |
| `begin(chat, by, *, opening=None)` | The opening turn, as a proposed take: a scenario's first opening (narrated to the table), else the launch card's first greeting spoken by its character, else the chat's premise. `opening` chooses another by its imported id (052): a launch-card greeting (`first`, `alt-2`, `group-1`; a group-only greeting is never a default but may be chosen) or a bound scenario's opening (`opening-2`); an id the chat lacks is `unknown_opening`, never the first one instead. `{{char}}` and `{{user}}` are filled. While that opening is not yet kept, `begin` again adds a fresh opening take for the cast as it is now. A chat with imported history (readable or not) already opened where it was written: `begin` is refused `already_begun`, and play goes on with `post` or `generate`. |
| `post(chat, by, text, *, mode="auto")` | The player's line (it needs words: `"..."` alone is `no_content`), routed by 008, checked by 003's `submit_contribution`, read by 032's `read_message`, and saved as a new turn before anything is generated. The same post retried onto its own untouched turn returns that turn. |
| `generate(chat, by, *, speakers=None, director=False)` | One new proposed take for the open turn (a `prompt` turn is opened when none is). Writes a `running` take with its planned calls first, then the result. |
| `keep(chat, by, take, *, acknowledge_unscreened=False)` | Keeps one proposed take with a shown line; the turn closes and a run still in flight lands stale. A take is kept only by the cast it was written for: once someone arrived or left, a listener changed hands, or a speaker became a player's character, it is refused `cast_changed` (ask again; `begin` asks for the opening again). A take written with unscreened director lore needs the acknowledgement. |
| `strike(chat, by, line_id)` | Takes a shown line of a kept take out of the transcript. |
| `pass_turn(chat, by)` | Closes the open turn with nothing kept. |
| `request(chat, by, kind, targets=(), *, direction=None)` | 016's mid-session request with no session-zero configuration: `pause`, or `skip`, `redirect` and `rephrase` naming lines, which are withdrawn (kept or proposed). A direction is recorded on the take. |
| `resume(chat, by)` | Lifts a pause; only the player who paused, or the library owner, may. |
| `choose(chat, by, turn)` | At a [forked chat](#forked-chats), the branch whose first turn is `turn` goes on; every other branch is set aside. Only the library owner may (`not_owner` for a player of a present character, `not_at_table` for anyone else, `no_chat` before anything is written for a chat this library cannot read); refused `not_forked`, `not_a_branch`, `turns_unreadable` or `turn_changed` otherwise. Returns the `Scene`. |

While play is paused nothing is begun, posted, generated, kept or passed (`paused`); marks and `resume` still work. A run already in flight when the pause lands writes its take, which waits for the resume. A pause is written only on the chat's last turn: if play went on meanwhile, it is refused `turn_changed`.

**Who may act.** `by` is a handle the same authority issued, trusted and human. Its principal must be the library owner or the controller of a present player character (`not_at_table`). Only the persona's controller may post for it (`not_your_character`). A persona whose recorded controller is the director (an actor reused from an NPC) is never played: `begin`, `post` and `generate` refuse `persona_not_player` until a player takes it back through 003. The principal is stored as `submitted_by` or `by`.

**Generating.** Speakers default to every present, director-controlled NPC with a voice; named speakers must be present (`not_present`), NPCs (`not_an_npc`) and voiced (`no_voice`). Each is briefed from its own knowledge (020, as of the newest sequence), its own pack built from this turn's snapshot, and only what it heard; silent present NPCs' voices go to the screen as `scene_voices`. A director reply is asked for explicitly, needs a bound scenario or a premise (`no_scenario`), and sees only what the table saw plus triggered lore. Every NPC line passes `review_narration` as voluntary speech by that NPC; a rejected line is dropped and its call marked `rejected`. A run claims its fence in the same step that publishes its running take, so an older run can never cancel the reroll that superseded it. The run re-checks its fence inside the commit: a run made stale keeps its usage and drops its lines. When a reroll supersedes a take still running and the older run lands first, the reroll reads the turn again and still writes its own take; a take that was itself superseded meanwhile is written stale. A take found `running` when the chat opens is reported `interrupted` and superseded by the next.

**Refusals** (`SceneRefused(reason)`). None writes anything, except that `generate` has already written its `running` take when its result is refused `turn_changed` (the next run supersedes that take, as an interrupted one). The reasons: `bad_handle`, `bad_runtime`, `invalid_field`, `chat_id_too_long`, `too_many_turns`, `no_chat`, `not_seated`, `not_authenticated`, `not_at_table`, `not_your_character`, `already_begun`, `no_opening`, `no_persona`, `persona_absent`, `persona_not_player`, `no_content`, `uncertain` (with the router's feedback), `not_in_this_slice` (commands, direction, interiority), 032's unread reasons, `turn_open`, `unknown_opening`, `no_runtime`, `session_zero_unreadable`, `party_unreadable`, `unassigned_actor`, `nothing_to_generate`, `too_many_takes`, `director_declined`, `no_scenario`, `not_present`, `not_an_npc`, `no_voice`, `not_proposed`, `empty_take`, `cast_changed`, `unscreened_lore`, `not_kept`, `unknown_line`, `no_targets`, `nothing_to_pause`, `already_paused`, `not_paused`, `not_yours`, `paused`, `no_open_turn`, `turns_unreadable`, `turn_fork` (two turns of one number after one turn: every write waits until the owner chooses a branch with `choose`), `not_owner`, `not_forked`, `not_a_branch` (`choose`), `turn_changed`, `device_identity` (a damaged device identity: no new turn); and 027's planning and briefing reasons, passed on unchanged.

## Storage and sync

- `turns/` is a history family: `load()` never reads it, and `store.campaign_turns(campaign)` lists it with per-file problems. A turn is created once (create-only, so two posts can never both become turn *n*: a library never creates turn *n* while its line holds a turn *n*, under any tag, and a new turn follows the line's last turn as read) and then changed only by compare-and-swap. A chat's turns are ordered by number, then an untagged turn first, then by device tag: one order on every device.
- A turn file of this chat that cannot be read stops every write to the chat, marks and pauses included (`turns_unreadable`), as does a turn whose predecessor is missing or cannot be told (`turn_gap`); a fork (`turn_fork`, every file readable) stops them too, refused `turn_fork`. A damaged turn of another chat does not.
- Every actor id a turn names stays taken for 107's allocators (`turn_instance_ids`), set-aside turns included, so a deleted actor's id never goes to a newcomer who would inherit what it heard.
- **Sync carries turns** ([SYNC](SYNC.md#what-is-synchronized)): a turn is a mutable record, so it follows the ordinary rows (copied over the agreed version, keep-both on concurrent edits), and it never lands without its chat record, the play scope, the turn it follows, the turn its mark names, the actors it names that the other device holds and the revisions its lines pin. Two devices playing one chat offline both write its next turn, as two files with two ids, and the next sync brings both to each device: the chat is then [forked](#forked-chats).

### Forked chats

<a id="forked-chats"></a>028's turn merge (`grimoire.scene.branches`, `SceneLibrary.choose`, `SceneRunner.choose`). Every turn names the turn it was played after, so a chat's turns are a tree, and its **line** is read from turn 1: while one turn follows the line's last turn, the line goes on. When two or more do, the chat is **forked** there:
- `open` reports it twice: `("turns", "<chat>:turn_fork")`, as before, and `("turn_fork", <detail>)` naming the fork's number and each branch by its first turn's id, its device tag (`untagged` for none), how many turns it holds from that number on, `this device` for this library's own, and `set aside` for one a choice set aside. `Scene.turns.fork` holds the same as data (`Fork`: `number`, `after`, `state`, `branches`; a `Branch`: `head`, `device`, `turns`, `set_aside`), for a UI to offer the choice. The scene shows every branch not set aside, in the one order, exactly as before 028's merge, and every write is refused `turn_fork`.
- **Nothing is chosen automatically.** The library owner calls `choose(chat, by, turn)` with the first turn of one branch (a player of a present character may not: choosing decides what everyone at the table heard). In one commit, by compare-and-swap on each file, every other branch head gets a `set_aside` mark naming the chosen head, and a mark on the chosen head is cleared. The marked heads and **every turn played after them** are set aside: kept on disk as they were, heard by no one, in no NPC's window and not in the table's view, taking no number in the line and no part in the fork check, with the actor ids they name still taken. A set-aside line can neither be struck nor withdrawn (`unknown_line`). Play goes on from the chosen branch. A pause on a turn the choice sets aside still holds: it is carried, in the same commit, onto the last turn of the chosen branch (each last turn, if it forked again later), so a choice never lifts a pause.
- **One mark per losing branch, on the turn it governs.** Two devices making the same choice write the same bytes, so they converge. A turn arriving later from a third device that was played after a set-aside turn is set aside too, by its `follows`. A new branch arriving after a choice (a third device's own turn *n*) makes the chat forked again, with the set-aside branch kept aside: choose again.
- **Marks that choose nothing are a conflict, never a guess.** Unless exactly one branch is unmarked and every mark names it (or, with more than one unmarked branch, every mark names one of them), the fork's state is `conflict` (`choices conflict` in the detail): no mark counts, every branch is shown, writes are refused `turn_fork`, and the owner chooses again. Two devices choosing differently offline each mark the other's branch: sync copies both marks (two files), so both devices show the same conflict, and neither silently flips to the other's choice. When both devices rewrote the same head (three branches, say), sync reports an ordinary keep-both conflict on that file, and each device reads its own marks until it is resolved.
- A fork already settled is never chosen again (`not_forked`). A later fork of the chosen branch is a fork again. A choice made further down a branch still holds when an earlier fork opens again (a third device's turn arriving late): that branch is shown without what the later choice set aside. The report names the chat's first fork only: a fork, or a conflict of choices, further down a branch is reported once the line reaches it.
- **A number played again.** A device that chose another device's branch plays the numbers its own set-aside turns hold again, so its own `<chat>.<n>-<tag>` id is taken: such a turn takes the first free **replay tag** instead, the first eight hex digits of the SHA-256 of `<tag>.<k>` (k = 1, 2, ...). A library with no identity has nothing unique to derive one from, so it refuses such a turn `device_identity`: give it an identity, then play on. A replayed turn's chat id is at most 88 characters, as for any tagged turn.

## Known limits

- **Merged turns.** A pause on a turn that a choice sets aside is carried onto the chosen line by `choose`; a paused turn that arrives after the choice and is set aside by its `follows` does not pause the chat. A choice whose marks conflict with a peer's on one file, or a turn whose predecessor is in a keep-both conflict, reads as this device's own version until sync's conflict is resolved ([SYNC](SYNC.md#played-turns-035-028s-turn-merge)). Each read of a chat's turns arranges them all, under the store lock for a write, so its cost grows with the chat.
- **One turn file per write.** A post, a take or a mark is one compare-and-swap; a concurrent writer is refused `turn_changed`. A new turn is created only while the previous turn is unchanged since it was read, so a pause written meanwhile is never played over. A rewrite keeps the envelope's owner and unknown fields. A run that loses that race is written again as a usage-only stale take, up to three times.
- **A reply to a whisper is spoken aloud.** An NPC answering a whisper speaks to the room, so if the model repeats what was whispered, everyone present hears it. Private NPC replies are 035 M2.
- **No control history.** `seat` records control for the chat's current actors; reassigning a character mid-chat is 003's.
- **Party.** The cast is 033's staged `SceneCast` (`Scene.staged`; `Scene.cast` is its `present`), staged with 033's `stage` under the chat id as the scene id, from the chat's active bound actors less 033's reserved members (stored away: left out, not a problem) and members whose membership is uncertain (left out and reported `party_uncertain`, fail closed). A member `stage` refuses is left out and reported by its reason: `missing_actor` (no instance file) or `instance_id_conflict` (its id is also used by another kind). While a relation file cannot be read no cast is staged (`cast_unstaged`, `relations_unreadable`): nobody is present, so no player plays a present character. The library owner is refused `party_unreadable` by `begin`, `post` and `generate` and `cast_changed` by `keep`; a player who is not the owner is refused `not_at_table` before that. Any party problem refuses generation. The cast is staged afresh on every call, never stored; 041's `check_cast` takes `Scene.staged`.
- **Budgets are per take, not per chat.** What bounds a turn's model spend is: the inference policy's per-turn call caps for its optional stages (a director reply needs `director_generation`) and one baseline call per speaking NPC, as 027 plans them; `max_attempts_per_call` retries per call; the runtime's `max_output_tokens` per attempt (027's default 400); the context `Budget` for each pack and the director's lore; and at most 16 takes per turn (`MAX_TAKES`). The caps apply to one take's plan (`<turn_id>.<take>`), so every reroll gets a fresh allowance: a turn can spend up to 16 times one take's caps. There is **no running spend cap** across takes, turns or chats, and usage is recorded in tokens only, with **no money cost**. A running spend cap and priced usage belong to [Turn Costs And Feature Flags](roadmap/sprints/turn-costs-and-feature-flags.md) (004) and [Model Routing And Budgeting](roadmap/sprints/model-routing-and-budgeting.md) (013).
- **History cost grows with the campaign.** Every runner call loads the library afresh (`store.load()`), reads and decodes every turn file of the campaign (every chat's, not only this one's), and reads the chat's imported transcript pages; a write lists the campaign's turn files again under the lock. Nothing is cached or indexed, so a call's cost grows roughly linearly with the campaign's played history: measured with synthetic turns on one development machine, `open` and one `post` plus `pass_turn` took about 0.05 s and 0.2 s at 10 turns, 0.5 s and 1.9 s at 500, and 1.1 s and 2.8 s at 1,000. There is no timing or tracing hook. A per-chat index or cache, and the measurements, are [Performance And Tracing](roadmap/sprints/performance-and-tracing.md)'s (048).
- **No `last_activity` update** per post (107's chat record would need a second write in the commit).
- **Imported history.** Every call decodes every page of the chat (a long SillyTavern chat costs a fraction of a second per call; see *History cost*), though only the last `MAX_HEARD` lines are used. It grants no knowledge: no 020 delivery is written for an imported line, as for a played one. Unreadable pages blank the history but stop no write, unlike an unreadable turn: an NPC answering without it misses lines, never hears one it should not, and once the pages read again the history counts as heard before turn 1. 026's V0.2.1 pages record no page count, so a lost last page of theirs goes unnoticed. 026 writes the pages of a chat it imported before 036 on a later run, possibly after the chat was played: the history then sits before turns already kept, and `begin`'s refusal could not apply. A SillyTavern import with no speaker mapped has no imported cast, so nobody hears its history. A V0.2.1 scene played with every reply under `**Grimoire:**` gives its NPCs only the player's posts: every reply is narration.
- **A missing source.** A bound card, lorebook or scenario that is gone (deleted while the app was closed) is reported as `("broken_source", <ref>)`, the exact ref the chat bound, alongside what follows from it (the character's `unnamed_actor` and `no_voice`). The turns are kept as they were, the chat still opens and takes posts, and nothing similarly named is bound in its place: a character without its card is silent (`nothing_to_generate`), a chat without its scenario has no director text (`no_scenario`).
- **The director reply's scenario text** is the scenario's premise, setting and instructions, or the chat's premise.
