# Scenes: the first playable text chat

**Status:** first slice of [Minimal Text Scene Runner](roadmap/sprints/minimal-text-scene-runner.md) (Sprint 035, Wave 5). Headless: there is no UI, streaming or async yet. It is three packages:
- `grimoire.scene` (pure): a played turn, its payload codec (`grimoire.scene-turn/1`), and what each listener heard;
- `grimoire.scenestore` (adapter): the `turns/` record family over one open `LibraryStore`;
- `grimoire.play` (adapter): `SceneRunner`, which plays one chat through [027](GENERATION.md)'s runtime.

All scenarios are synthetic and every model call is scripted.

## What it does

**Proving scenario** (`backend/tests/test_scene_proof.py`). Two chats are played, closed and opened again with a new store, authority and model runtime, as an app restart would:
- **A migrated V1 campaign.** [026](IMPORT_V1.md)'s Drowned Ledger import: Winifred is the player's character; Mara speaks in an authored voice and Seraphine in the voice derived from her imported card. Over three turns the player asks a question, whispers to Mara, pauses and resumes, redirects one of Seraphine's lines, saves a post and closes the app before any call. After the restart the post is still there and is answered. Mara's prompt holds the whisper; Seraphine's holds neither the whisper nor her own withdrawn line.
- **A world-less card chat.** [037](CARDS.md)'s Mara Vell card with its lorebook, and Anya Rook as the persona. The card's first greeting opens the chat; asking about the lighthouse triggers the card's lore in Mara's prompt. After a whisper, a second card (Tamsin Hale) is attached mid-chat. Tamsin's first prompt holds what was said since she arrived, and nothing before.
- **A scenario-only chat** (Storm over Port Vell) with no NPC: the only generation is a director reply the player asks for, and the policy allows.

**Across real restarts** (`backend/tests/test_scene_restart.py`). The same three chats, with every phase in a new Python process that ends with `os._exit` (nothing closed, as when the app is killed), one of them killed inside `generate` after its `running` take was written: each chat goes on exactly where it stopped, the killed run is reported `interrupted` and superseded, and the scenario-only chat gets a new director reply.

Each speaking NPC gets its own call, with its own voice, its own [031](CONTEXT.md) pack and only the lines it heard. Every call's attempts and token usage are stored with its take, even when the turn halted or went stale.

## A turn

`TurnRef(campaign_id, turn_id)` is `campaign:<c>#turn/<t>`, stored at `campaigns/<c>/turns/<t>.json` in the scope-record envelope ([STORAGE](STORAGE.md)). `turn_id` is `<chat_id>.<n:06d>`, numbered from 1 per chat. On a library with a device identity ([SYNC](SYNC.md)) a new turn's id also carries that device's tag, `<chat_id>.<n:06d>-<tag>` (`chatstore.device_tag`, the tag 028 gives new chat actors and 034 new items, [DEVICE_SYNC](DEVICE_SYNC.md)), recorded in the payload as `device`, so two paired devices that each play turn *n* offline write two turns with two ids, never one id with two contents (which 032 would read as one message, and 020 as one immutable record, once milestone 2 writes deliveries under it). A library never given an identity numbers turns as before. A turn keeps its id for good: turns played before `identify` are still read, rewritten under their own id, and play goes on from them with tagged turns; a turn planned before `identify` and written after it is refused `turn_changed`, and a damaged identity refuses every new turn (`device_identity`) rather than mint untagged ids. A payload without `device` (written before the tag existed) reads as untagged. Both forms fit 032's 104-character message ids: an untagged turn's chat id is at most 97 characters, as before tags, so every turn saved before them still reads; a tagged turn's is at most 88. So a chat whose id is 89 to 97 characters long keeps its history and plays on a library with no identity, but takes no new turn once its library is identified (`chat_id_too_long`). A chat holds at most 999,999 turns (`too_many_turns`).

A `TurnRecord` holds:
- `origin`: `post` (a player's post), `prompt` (the table asked the cast to go on), `opening` (a card's greeting or a scenario's opening), or `imported` (reserved; 036's imported history lives in its own `transcripts/` family);
- `post`: the player's line, who submitted it, its kind (`speech` or `action`), channel (one of 032's: `speech`, `whisper`, `telepathy`), addressees (a private channel needs at least one), **audience**, and whether it is `withheld` (an action its sender alone hears);
- `takes`: up to 16. A take is `running`, `proposed`, `kept`, `withdrawn`, `stale` or `superseded`. It records its cast (and, played on a [stage](#the-stage-041), `on_stage`: the part of the cast the stage had present; `Take.present` is that, else the whole cast), its lines, every planned call with its status and attempts, the policy's declined calls, any unscreened director lore, a redirect's direction, and who chose it;
- `intentions`: [039's choice](#npc-intentions-039) for each speaking director NPC with an agenda, one per take and NPC (written only when there is one; a turn written before it has none);
- `paused` (and, kept only for `resume`, who paused), and `passed_by`;
- `follows`: the id of the turn it was played after (`null` for turn 1), and `set_aside`: [028's mark](#forked-chats) on a branch head that does not go on (`{"chosen": <turn id>, "by": <principal>}`, or `null`). A turn written before these fields has neither: it is placed after the turn before it of its own device, else the untagged one, else the only one, and never by a guess past that (`turn_gap`).

A turn is open until one take is kept or somebody passes it. Lines are `<turn_id>.<take>.<n>` and are `shown`, `withdrawn` or `struck`. Unknown top-level payload keys survive a rewrite; inside a known structure an unknown or malformed field is refused, never dropped.

**A turn is transcript, never world state.** A kept line is what the table chose to show. No event, belief, relation, effect or ruling is written from it, so nothing said here becomes canon a later rule could read. The one way into world state is an effect proposed in structured form and accepted with every key ([Accepting an effect](#accepting-an-effect)).

## Who heard what

- A post's audience is 032's `transmit` receivers plus the sender. Room speech with nobody else present is heard by the speaker alone. An action that is no message at all (032's `not_directed`) is seen by everyone present. An action 032 cannot rule out as a private message (`unresolved_private`: a note, an aside under the breath, a nod) is **withheld**: its sender alone hears it, and it is in neither an NPC's window nor the table's. A message aimed at someone 032 cannot resolve is refused with 032's reason (`unknown_addressee`, `unclear_content`, `ambiguous_addressee`, ...); nothing 032 cannot read ever falls back to a public line.
- An NPC line is heard by everyone present, even when it answers a whisper. With a [stage](#the-stage-041), "present" is physical: a post, an NPC line and a visible action reach only those in earshot or in sight, and an off-stage cast member neither posts nor speaks. A narrator line is heard by the table (the present player characters), never by an NPC.
- **Audience is fixed when a line is spoken.** An actor attached later was in no earlier audience, so it hears nothing that came before. A character handed to the director later keeps what it heard as a player.
- Proposals, withdrawn lines and struck lines are heard by no one. A reroll never hears the take it replaces. A line with no words (`...`) is heard by no one either, and does not take a place in the window.
- **Imported history comes first.** A chat's [036](TRANSCRIPTS.md) transcript pages (a migrated V0.2.1 scene, a SillyTavern import) are its history before turn 1, read on every call and never copied into turns (`Scene.imported`, `grimoire.scene.imported_history`, read by `grimoire.play.history`). The pages say who wrote each post, never who heard it, so the runner decides that by one rule: **the actors the chat was imported with** (its roster at sequence 0: an importer starts the chat with its whole cast and never rebinds it) heard every shown post, and an actor attached later heard none of it, as with any late arrival. Only a post's chosen variant is heard.
  - A post by the player, a character, or a source's generated reply that names its speaker (V0.2.1's `assistant` with a label such as `Mara`) is heard by that cast and seen by the table.
  - Narration is the table's only, never an NPC's: a `narrator` post, and V0.2.1's narrator (the reserved `**Grimoire:**` speaker, which 026 writes as an `assistant` post with no label).
  - A `system` notice (a V0.2.1 synthetic post, a dice roll), a `hidden` post, an unchosen swipe and a post with no chosen text are nobody's.
  - A line's speaker is the cast member's current name when the post names its actor, else the source's label; a player post with neither (V0.2.1's `**You:**`) is the imported persona's, by name (`Player` if there is none), and a label with no words (`???`) falls back to the role.
  - Imported lines take places in the same window as played lines; only the last `MAX_HEARD` of each view are built.
  - If any of the chat's pages cannot be read, the numbering has a gap, or a SillyTavern import's page 1 says it wrote more pages than there are (`missing_pages`), **nobody hears any of it** (fail closed: a listener would otherwise hear part of the history, unmarked), and `open` reports `imported_history_unreadable`. A campaign `transcripts` folder that cannot be walked (a link, a file) may hide any chat's pages, so it does the same for every chat of the campaign; a look-alike sibling (`Transcripts`) does the same for a chat with pages, and is only reported (`transcripts`) for a chat with none.
- `heard_for` gives an NPC's window (the last `MAX_HEARD` lines, each capped at 027's `MAX_LINE_CHARS`); it is both its brief's `heard` and its pack's `scene_text`. `table_lines` is the director's view: the imported history the table saw, every post but a private one, and every kept line (of a take played on a 041 stage, only the lines some player character as of its turn, `Take.players`, is in the audience of: [The stage](#the-stage-041)).

## The runner

`SceneRunner(store, authority, handle, *, owner, runtime=None, policy=None, secrets=(), voices=None, fence=None, budget=DEFAULT_BUDGET, labeller=None, label_handle=None, adjudication=None, effects=None, agendas=(), stages=None, tracer=None, clock=time.perf_counter)`.
- `handle` is a trusted human handle `authority` issued (the library owner's). It writes turn files and records control at `seat`.
- `runtime` is the app's `GenerationRuntime`: a trusted app handle, the configured provider only, no approvals asked. Without one, nothing is generated (`no_runtime`).
- `policy` caps the calls a turn may plan; a director reply needs `director_generation`.
- `secrets` are screened in every brief. With none, only 031's director-lore markers are screened.
- `voices` are authored profiles; an imported character with none derives its voice from its card (027/026), and one with no example lines speaks anyway.
- `fence` makes an older run of the same chat stale.
- `labeller` is the app's `LabelRuntime` (its own untrusted provider handle, every attempt approved) and `label_handle` the untrusted handle `authority` issued for proposing labels. Without both, nothing is labelled (`no_labeller`); see [Labelling an action](#labelling-an-action).
- `adjudication` is the library's 029 `AdjudicationLibrary` (on this `store`, `bad_adjudication` otherwise), over the app's own trusted adjudication key, issued by this runner's authority: its principal is an `app:` one, never the labeller's (`label_handle` or the labeller runtime's) and never a player's or the owner's (`bad_adjudication` otherwise). Without it nothing is framed or decided (`no_adjudication`); see [Confirming and deciding an action](#confirming-and-deciding-an-action).
- `effects` is the library's 021 `TurnEffectsLibrary`, on this `store` and on this runner's own `handle` (the owner's: the outcome key), `bad_effects` otherwise. Without it no effect is proposed (`no_effects`); see [Accepting an effect](#accepting-an-effect).
- `agendas` are 039's director-authored `Agenda` values, exactly `Agenda` and at most one per actor (`bad_agenda` otherwise), copied when the runner is made; see [NPC intentions](#npc-intentions-039).
- `stages` is the app's 041 `StageSource` (`grimoire.play.stages`): any object whose `latest(chat)` gives the chat's latest `Stage`, or `None` for a chat with none (`bad_stages` when it has no `latest`). Without one, or for a chat it has no stage for, the cast alone decides who hears what, as before; see [The stage](#the-stage-041).
- `tracer` (048) is handed one `grimoire.tracing.Trace` per public call: where its wall time went and, for `generate` and `label`, what each model call used. `clock` times it. Without a tracer nothing is timed. See [PERFORMANCE](PERFORMANCE.md).

Every method reads the chat afresh (one `LibraryView`, 022's chat snapshot, the turns, 010b's recorded control, 033's party) and writes at most one turn file. Reading the turns afresh reads every turn file of this chat and hashes its bytes, but decodes only the files whose bytes changed since this runner last read them; another chat's turn files are not read at all (048).

| Method | What it does |
|---|---|
| `open(chat)` | The `Scene` as it stands: turns, cast, persona, names, controls and problems (a damaged turn, an `interrupted` run, a bound source that is gone: `broken_source` with its exact ref, imported pages that cannot all be read: `imported_history_unreadable` with each page's problem (or `transcripts` for the campaign's folder, in a chat with none), a cast member with `no_voice`, a party member left out (`party_uncertain`, `missing_actor`, `instance_id_conflict`, `cast_unstaged`), a persona the director controls: `persona_not_player`). Writes nothing. |
| `seat(chat, player)` | Records control for actors with none: the persona to `player`, every other bound character to the director. A recorded controller is never changed. |
| `begin(chat, by, *, opening=None)` | The opening turn, as a proposed take: a scenario's first opening (narrated to the table), else the launch card's first greeting spoken by its character, else the chat's premise. `opening` chooses another by its imported id (052): a launch-card greeting (`first`, `alt-2`, `group-1`; a group-only greeting is never a default but may be chosen) or a bound scenario's opening (`opening-2`); an id the chat lacks is `unknown_opening`, never the first one instead. `{{char}}` and `{{user}}` are filled. While that opening is not yet kept, `begin` again adds a fresh opening take for the cast as it is now. A chat with imported history (readable or not) already opened where it was written: `begin` is refused `already_begun`, and play goes on with `post` or `generate`. |
| `post(chat, by, text, *, mode="auto")` | The player's line (it needs words: `"..."` alone is `no_content`), routed by 008, checked by 003's `submit_contribution`, read by 032's `read_message`, and saved as a new turn before anything is generated. The same post retried onto its own untouched turn returns that turn. Text the router reads as `pause`, `resume`, `skip` or `redirect` is run as that request instead ([Pause, skip and redirect](#pause-skip-and-redirect-016)). |
| `generate(chat, by, *, speakers=None, director=False)` | One new proposed take for the open turn (a `prompt` turn is opened when none is). Writes a `running` take with its planned calls first, then the result. |

A take is kept only by the cast it was written for: once someone arrived or left, a listener changed hands, or a speaker became a player's character, it is refused `cast_changed` (ask again; `begin` asks for the opening again). A take written with unscreened director lore needs the acknowledgement. |
| `strike(chat, by, line_id)` | Takes a shown line of a kept take out of the transcript: for a player, only a line a character of theirs heard (`not_heard`, [Pause, skip and redirect](#pause-skip-and-redirect-016)); the owner, any. |
| `pass_turn(chat, by)` | Closes the open turn with nothing kept. |
| `request(chat, by, kind, targets=(), *, direction=None)` | 016's mid-session request with no session-zero configuration, for the table's authenticated audience: `pause`, or `skip`, `redirect` and `rephrase` naming lines, which are withdrawn (kept or proposed); a redirect leaves a line of accepted canon alone, and a skip passes over it without touching the effect ([Pause, skip and redirect](#pause-skip-and-redirect-016)). A direction is recorded on the take. |
| `resume(chat, by)` | Lifts a pause: the player who paused lifts their own, the library owner every one (on the line or set aside, in one commit). Returns the chat's last turn. |
| `choose(chat, by, turn)` | At a [forked chat](#forked-chats), the branch whose first turn is `turn` goes on; every other branch is set aside. Only the library owner may (`not_owner` for a player of a present character, `not_at_table` for anyone else, `no_chat` before anything is written for a chat this library cannot read); refused `not_forked`, `not_a_branch`, `turns_unreadable` or `turn_changed` otherwise. Returns the `Scene`. |
| `label(chat, by)` | Proposes 029 labels for the open turn's action post: which offered facts bear on it and what is at stake. See [Labelling an action](#labelling-an-action). |
| `confirm(chat, by)` | The acting player frames the open action exactly as its latest proposed labelling says. Returns the `Outcome` when the rules decide it at once, else `None`. See [Confirming and deciding an action](#confirming-and-deciding-an-action). |
| `decide(chat, by, result, *, basis="adjudicated", cites=(), consequences=())` | Decides a framed, uncertain action: the director adjudicates it, or its player consents to a setback. |
| `outcome(chat)` | The open turn's recorded outcome, if its action was decided. |
| `propose_effect(chat, by, turn, summary, operations=(), holdings=None, *, abilities=())` | Proposes a kept turn's or a decided action's effect as structured 021 operations and holdings lines. See [Accepting an effect](#accepting-an-effect). |
| `approve_effect(chat, by, turn, number)` / `endorse_effect(chat, by, turn, number, fingerprint)` | The director's key and a player's key on that effect; whichever comes last accepts it into 021. |
| `recover_effects(chat, by, *, abort=False, endorsers=(), own=False)` | Settles each of the chat's acceptances that was cut short, by what landed (030); the owner only (`own`: an untagged one with nothing here is this library's). |

While play is paused nothing is begun, posted, generated, kept or passed (`paused`); marks and `resume` still work. A run already in flight when the pause lands writes its take, which waits for the resume. A pause is written only on the chat's last turn: if play went on meanwhile, it is refused `turn_changed`. Play is paused while **any** turn of the chat holds an open pause, on the line or set aside by a [fork choice](#forked-chats) (`ChatTurns.pauses`), so a merge never lifts one: two devices' pauses can both stand after sync, and each stays until the player who made it, or the owner, resumes; play goes on once none stands.

**Who may act.** `by` is a handle the same authority issued, trusted and human. Its principal must be the library owner or the controller of a present player character (`not_at_table`). Only the persona's controller may post for it (`not_your_character`). A persona whose recorded controller is the director (an actor reused from an NPC) is never played: `begin`, `post` and `generate` refuse `persona_not_player` until a player takes it back through 003. The principal is stored as `submitted_by` or `by`.

**Generating.** Speakers default to every present, director-controlled NPC with a voice; named speakers must be present (`not_present`), NPCs (`not_an_npc`) and voiced (`no_voice`). Each is briefed from its own knowledge (020, as of the newest sequence), its own pack built from this turn's snapshot, and only what it heard; silent present NPCs' voices go to the screen as `scene_voices`. A speaker with a 039 agenda is also briefed with what it chose to pursue for this take ([NPC intentions](#npc-intentions-039)). A director reply is asked for explicitly, needs a bound scenario or a premise (`no_scenario`), and sees only what the table saw plus triggered lore. Every NPC line passes `review_narration` as voluntary speech by that NPC; a rejected line is dropped and its call marked `rejected`. A run claims its fence in the same step that publishes its running take, so an older run can never cancel the reroll that superseded it. The run re-checks its fence inside the commit: a run made stale keeps its usage and drops its lines. When a reroll supersedes a take still running and the older run lands first, the reroll reads the turn again and still writes its own take; a take that was itself superseded meanwhile is written stale. A take found `running` when the chat opens is reported `interrupted` and superseded by the next.

<a id="turn-room"></a>**Room in the turn file.** A turn is one file, and the store reads back no file over 8 MiB (`MAX_TURN_BYTES`); `SceneLibrary.write` refuses a turn past it `turn_too_large` before anything is written, and `SceneLibrary.size` measures one. Before the running take is written (so before any call is billed), `generate` refuses `turn_too_large` unless the turn with its running take and intentions leaves room for each planned call's line and attempts and the take's own fields (`LINE_BYTES`, `ATTEMPT_BYTES` per allowed attempt, `TAKE_BYTES`): keep a take, or pass, and play on in a new turn. Should a finished take still not fit (a turn grown on another version meanwhile), its usage lands anyway: first without its lines (the take is `proposed` and `halted` with no lines, each call that gave one `rejected` as `turn_too_large`, so the table generates again), then also without that take's intentions. Only a turn too large for even that is refused `turn_too_large`, its running take left as written (reported `interrupted`).

**Refusals** (`SceneRefused(reason)`). ## NPC intentions (039)

<a id="npc-intentions-039"></a>[039](GOALS.md#in-play-035)'s deterministic goals in play (035 milestone 2). An NPC's agenda costs no model call: it changes only what the NPC's one ordinary call is briefed with.

**Choosing.** For every take, `generate` chooses afresh for each speaker that has an agenda (a director NPC: a player character is never a speaker), from the very `ActorKnowledge` its brief is built from, as of the newest sequence when the take is planned. The take's cast is split in two for that NPC: whom it recognises (`present`) and whom it does not (`unrecognised`): an actor whose name, as the NPC's brief was given it, states a secret the NPC has not learned (a disguised clerk's real name, registered as a `SecretFact` in `secrets`), so the brief labels them "Someone" ([GENERATION](GENERATION.md#intentions-039)'s `unrecognised`, which labels each actor exactly as the brief does: by its name, else by the speaker its heard lines carry). An actor the brief has no label for at all is unrecognised too (fail closed), and on a 041 stage so is anyone on stage the NPC cannot see ([The stage](#the-stage-041)). An unrecognised actor is absent to the choice: an approach needing them is unmet. The brief then carries the choice ([GENERATION](GENERATION.md#intentions-039)): the goal's aim, the chosen approach's action and the rules of the limits it is refusing. A take reroll chooses again, so it follows whatever the NPC has learned since.

**Checked before use, fail closed.** Before an agenda is used, `check_agenda` runs against the campaign ledger's proposition ids and the campaign's readable actor instances. An agenda naming a proposition or an actor the campaign lacks refuses the take with 039's reason (`unknown_proposition`, `unknown_actor`): a condition that never holds would let a limit lapse silently. A ledger with a quarantined record or any problem refuses an agenda NPC's take too (`ledger_damaged`): the lost record may be the very belief a limit watches for. Either way nothing is written and no call is made; the table can still name other speakers (`speakers=`), and an NPC without an agenda plays on as before. `choose`'s own refusals pass on unchanged.

**Recorded on the turn.** The running take is written with each choice beside it (`TurnRecord.intentions`), so a crash keeps the plan, and the landed take keeps it, also when it lands stale (a newer run claimed the chat) or on another version of its turn (a resolved sync conflict) as a stale take. An `Intention` holds its take, the NPC, the knowledge sequence it was chosen as of, the recognised and unrecognised cast (together exactly those present for the take, `Take.present`: its on-stage cast when played on a stage, else its whole cast; a take written before stages has no `on_stage`, so its intentions keep the whole cast), the goal and approach pursued (none when the NPC could pursue nothing), the limits it was refusing, the approaches as weighed (`IntentionOption`: its outcome, the conditions unmet, the limits forbidding it) and 039's `explain` lines, so a reload says why the NPC acted without the agenda at hand. Recorded are every approach tried up to and including the chosen one (with nothing chosen, all of them) and, after it, those a limit forbids: what the NPC refuses is always kept. The others after the choice (`outranked`, or `unmet`) are only counted (`omitted`), so an agenda's size never multiplies into every take; `explain` names only the choice, so it says the same. It is stored as `intentions` in the turn payload (a list, sorted by take and actor), written only when there is one: an older turn reads as none, and an older build keeps the key as an unknown field when it rewrites the turn. A malformed or unknown field inside one is refused, never dropped.

**No one authors an agenda in play.** Agendas are the runner's configuration (`agendas`), director-authored and copied when the runner is made. Nothing a player posts, a model replies or a narrator says becomes or changes one, and a recorded `Intention` is never read back as an agenda or a choice: it is an account of one. Its lines name actors and propositions by id, so they are director-side: in no NPC's window, not in the table's view, though on the turn file the owner's devices hold.

**Known limits.**
- Agendas are not stored: the app passes them to every runner it makes (storing them as a campaign record family is 039's next slice).
- The turn is not given to the NPCs whose `Choice.acts`: speakers are chosen as before, so an NPC with nothing to pursue still speaks. That scene rule is not applied.
- Recognition reads only the labels the secret screen hides: sight, sound and disguise records are 041's. An actor the brief has no label for (no name, never heard) counts as unrecognised, so an approach needing them stays unmet until they are named or speak.
- With nothing chosen, every approach is recorded with its unmet conditions, so a large agenda that can do nothing fills a turn file fastest; the room check then refuses another take (`turn_too_large`) rather than write a file no reader accepts. The room per call is a fixed estimate, not a measure of the reply; the landing fallback covers a take that outgrows it.
- No reply is checked against its NPC's intention: a line breaking a refusal is kept like any other (047's referee).
- A planner's wish (`desired`) is never passed: no planner is wired in.

## Labelling an action

The first step of 029's fiction-first adjudication for a player character's action (035 milestone 2). Labelling frames nothing and decides nothing: it proposes the labels a frame would carry, on the action's own turn.

**Who asks.** The acting character's player, or the library owner, by pressing for it (`label(chat, by)`). Only the open turn's post is labelled, only when it is an action (`not_an_action`) the table saw (a withheld action is `private_action`: its labelling would need a private frame), and only for a present player character (`not_a_player_action`); another player is refused `not_controller`. An action already framed is refused `already_framed`. As for generation, nothing is labelled while the chat's scope holds a session zero this slice cannot read (`session_zero_unreadable`). A press may name a 040 obstacle the action attempts (`label(chat, by, obstacle=...)`): the labeller then also chooses the approach ([Attempting an obstacle](#attempting-an-obstacle-040)).

**What the labeller sees.** One `labelling` call ([INFERENCE](INFERENCE.md)), planned for the scene with no actor (no model call decides for a player character) under its own plan turn, `<turn_id>.l<n>`. Its prompt holds the action, the last 12 lines the acting character heard (context only, not citable; narration is the table's, not the character's, so no narrator line is among them) and a numbered list of facts from the acting character's 031 pack, **packed for its present controller** (`player_context`, [CONTEXT](CONTEXT.md#packing-for-a-player-characters-controller)): their description and card sections, their perceptions, the propositions they believe on their present player's own authority (020's `controller_knowledge`, `player_knowledge` for this character alone; where the director revised the player's conclusion since, the player's latest own belief), and the lore their own window and that knowledge trigger. A belief someone else authored (an NPC-era conclusion the player inherited, or one a previous player drew) is not in the pack at all, so it is never offered, never ranks and never triggers lore; the labeller checks the believed propositions against that projection again before offering them (fail closed). The pack's `scene_text` and the prompt's 12 lines are the same window, so a whisper to others, a line spoken behind a shut door on a 041 stage, and a line 016's skip or redirect withdrew are in neither, and its `present` is whom the character sees. A character whose control cannot be confirmed is not labelled (`not_a_player_action`), another character's ambiguous control does not touch this one's memory, and a character whose knowledge 020 cannot project for its player is not labelled either (`knowledge_unprojected`): no labels over a memory-less pack. A quarantined ledger is not refused (`knowledge_quarantined`, as for an NPC). It never sees a secret, an NPC's private knowledge, another player's character's knowledge, a table event or director lore.

**What its reply may say.** Only `{"factors": [{"fact": "f1", "bearing": "hinders"}], "stakes": ["..."]}`: offered facts cited by number with a bearing (`favours`, `hinders`, `prevents`), and short stakes in its own words (at most 300 characters each, 32 factors and 16 stakes). A factor's text, sequence and source are the offered fact's, never the reply's, so a labeller cannot invent a difficulty. Anything else (another key, a fact not offered, a repeated fact, a decision) refuses the reply whole: nothing is repaired or partly kept.

**What is written.** The turn records each labelling (`labels`, at most 8 per action): first `running` with its planned call, before the call, so a crash leaves the plan on record (a crash mid-call loses that call's usage, as for a take, and the next labelling supersedes it); then `proposed` with its factors and stakes, or `refused` with why (the call's status, or the reply's refusal reason). Every attempt's usage is kept either way. Like a take, a labelling is started only while no pause or sync conflict stands and its turn is still on the chat's line (`turn_conflict` before the call, `turn_changed` under the lock), and once its call has run it always lands over its own running labelling, wherever a pause, a fork choice or a sync conflict put its turn meanwhile, so its usage is never lost. A new labelling supersedes any running or proposed one (`superseded`, labels dropped). The action is framed at the sequence after the newest knowledge, so every cited fact was established before it.

**Two handles** (the requester's decision in the Wave 6 audit). The labeller's provider calls run on its own untrusted handle, each attempt approved by the app for the press that asked. Before a labelling holding anything read from a reply is written, `label_handle` must be allowed to `propose` that turn's canonical payload at its address; if not, the labelling is written `refused` with `not_authorized` and no labels. The owner's handle then writes the turn file, as for every turn ([TOOL_AUTHORITY](TOOL_AUTHORITY.md)).

**Known limits.**
- A hindrance the acting character does not know of is never labelled: the facts offered are theirs. A referee's view of the scene belongs to the decision, not to labelling.
- Kept lines are not facts: what an NPC said is offered only as context, never as a citable fact, until 020 records it as knowledge.
- Labelling runs on a press, not on every post.
- At most 64 facts are offered, in pack order; a crowded pack may leave a relevant fact out.
- The controller's pack keeps what the character witnessed as an NPC (its perceptions and reports carry no author) and its own card however it was authored; only conclusions are sorted by author ([CONTEXT](CONTEXT.md#packing-for-a-player-characters-controller)). It is the labeller's alone: no pack is shown to the player yet.

## Confirming and deciding an action

The rest of 029's adjudication for a player character's action (035 milestone 2): the labelling becomes a frame, and the frame a decision, before anything narrates the result.

**The player confirms, unedited** (the requester's decision in Wave 6). `confirm(chat, by)` frames exactly the turn's latest labelling, and only while it is `proposed` (`not_proposed`): nothing in it can be edited, so a wrong labelling is fixed by labelling again (the acting player or the owner asks). Only the acting character's controller confirms (`not_controller`, the owner included), and only while that player still controls the character who posted (`control_changed`). A withheld action is never framed (`private_action`), and while play is paused nothing is confirmed or decided (`paused`). An action framed and decided already is `already_framed`, and a framed action is never labelled again.

**What 029 records.** The frame's attempt is the turn id, at the labelling's moment (the campaign's next sequence, as [Accepting an effect](#accepting-an-effect) allocates it, the chat and the turn), with the post's words as the intent (at most 4,000 characters), the labelling's factors and stakes, and `035 labelling <n>, confirmed by the acting player` as its provenance. It is backed by the player's own contribution (the post: its turn id, actor and `submitted_by`) and given with the player's own trusted handle, as 029 requires for a player character; the adjudication key records it. 029 checks the frame first, writing nothing (a refusal there leaves the labelling proposed). The labelling is then written `framed` on the turn (compare-and-swap: a relabel that lands meanwhile refuses the confirm `turn_changed`, a keep or pass that closed the turn meanwhile refuses it `no_open_turn`, and a pause that landed meanwhile refuses it `paused`; either way nothing reaches 029), and recorded in 029 after. Once framed the turn is never relabelled, so a confirm cut short by 029's ledger failing (`adjudication_unreadable`, `adjudication_unavailable`) or a crash finishes on the next `confirm`, with the same frame; meanwhile the turn is undecided. If 029 refuses the frame for good after the mark (the character's card deleted meanwhile), or the character changed hands before 029 recorded it, the labelling is written `refused` with that reason and the action goes back to unframed play. That rollback reads 029 again and writes the turn under the store lock 029 records frames under, so a concurrent confirm that recorded the frame keeps its turn marker. Two confirms at once may record the same frame twice (two equal revisions); the second returns the first's outcome.

**Deciding.** 029 assesses the frame at once. An action the rules decide is decided at once, by rule, with the adjudication key: `straightforward` succeeds (citing every favouring factor) and `impossible` fails (citing every preventing factor), and `confirm` returns its `Outcome`. An `uncertain` action waits (`confirm` returns `None`) for `decide`:
- `basis="adjudicated"`: the director (the library owner) chooses the result, citing the frame's factors and naming the stakes that come true (`not_director` for anyone else).
- `basis="consent"`: the acting player chooses a setback for their own character, a result worse than the best the frame allows, with their own trusted handle as their consent (`not_controller` for anyone else).

029 checks every rule (a success or partial cites a favouring factor, a failure or partial a hindering one, a partial names its consequences) and its refusal reason is passed on. Only a framed action is decided (`not_framed`); `basis` is `adjudicated` or `consent` (`invalid_field`).

**Narration waits for the decision.** While the open turn's action is framed and undecided, `generate` and `keep` are refused `undecided_attempt`, so no take is written for it and none is kept. A take written before the confirm never heard the outcome, so the write that frames the turn withdraws every proposed take and supersedes a running one (its run lands `stale`): none of them can be kept, and the table generates again once the action is decided. Once decided, the outcome is one heard line (speaker `Outcome`). The director reply sees the table's line: "<name>'s action (<intent>) succeeds | succeeds at a cost | fails. What comes of it: <consequences>. Because: <cited factors>.", cut to one heard line (2,000 characters); the player saw all of it when they confirmed. An NPC that saw the action hears only "The action succeeds | succeeds at a cost | fails.": the causes are the acting character's own facts, the stakes are the labeller's words, and the action's own line already names the actor as that NPC knows them. The outcome stays where it was heard: in every later turn's window it follows the action's post, for whoever heard that post (the table's line for the director, the bare result for an NPC and for the acting character's own labelling window). A framed turn is read only with the adjudication key (`no_adjudication`). An action that was never confirmed binds nothing: it narrates as before.

**Known limits.**
- **Narration is not checked against the outcome.** A take that narrates the opposite result is not refused here; checking narration against 029's resolution is the Narration Continuity Referee's (047), and turning narration into effects is the effects part of this milestone.
- **An NPC hears no cost.** It hears that the action succeeded at a cost, not which; the director's reply narrates what came of it.
- **A director who plays.** The library owner adjudicates even an action of a character they control (one person playing alone is both); 102's several players will need a director who is not the acting player.
- **The moment is the labelling's.** The frame's sequence is the campaign's next one when the action was labelled (after the newest knowledge and every sequence already used), not when it was posted; a relabel after new knowledge may cite it. The player confirms each labelling.
- **One adjudication key frames and decides.** The runner, not the key, decides who frames (the acting player, on their own contribution and handle) and who decides (the rules, the director, or the player's consent), so the key's holder is the app alone.
- **The intent is the post as written**, cut to 4,000 characters; nothing summarises it.
- **No ability is framed.** The runner's frames carry no 025 ability, so 029 never assesses one `clarify` (an ability that needs a ruling); framing a power, and its ruling, is 040's and this milestone's later part.
- **Passing the turn** with an undecided frame leaves the attempt undecided in 029; nothing narrates it later. An attempt at an obstacle that can still be decided is decided first instead (`attempt_undecided`), and one that cannot is withdrawn from its obstacle for good ([below](#attempting-an-obstacle-040)).

## Attempting an obstacle (040)

An action can be an attempt at a 040 [open obstacle](OBSTACLES.md) (035 milestone 2). The runner is then given the library's `ObstacleLibrary` (`SceneRunner(..., obstacles=...)`), on the same store and the same adjudication key as its 029 library (`bad_obstacles` otherwise, and without an adjudication library).

**The press names the obstacle.** `label(chat, by, obstacle="<obstacle id>")`: which obstacle an action is aimed at is the player's (or the owner's) choice when they ask for labels, never the model's. Before any call is planned, the obstacle must be open to an attempt: known (`unknown_obstacle`), not overcome (`already_overcome`) nor diverged (`obstacle_diverged`), and with no fact or stake id that labelling itself hands out (`f<n>`, `s<n>`) and no suggestion named `novel` (`obstacle_ids_clash`), so the author's labels and the labeller's can never be confused in a frame. An obstacle whose latest attempt is framed and still undecided is `attempt_pending`, as its library would refuse the frame (the director decides that attempt first); one that cannot be decided now (its actor changed hands, or its frame was never written) is superseded as 040 says. Under the store lock, before the running labelling is written, its moment must come after every authored fact (`premature`) and after the obstacle's latest attempt (`out_of_order`). A refusal writes nothing and makes no call. Without an obstacle library, naming one is `no_obstacles`.

**The labeller chooses the approach, and only that.** Its prompt adds the obstacle's goal and its suggested approaches (id and text), never the obstacle's facts or stakes. Its reply is `{"approach": "<id>" | "novel", "factors": [...], "stakes": [...]}`: a suggestion's id, or `novel` for one the author never suggested (it is told never to refuse an approach for being unlisted). An approach that is neither is `unknown_approach`, and a suggestion with stakes listed is `suggested_stakes`. It still decides nothing: the decider is 029, through `confirm` and `decide`, as for any action. The frame's obstacle part is then the author's, added by rule (040's `required_factors`):
- **a suggestion:** exactly the facts the author said bear on it, with the author's bearing, beside the labeller's own factors, and exactly the suggestion's risks as stakes, with their authored text;
- **a novel approach:** every fact the author said bears on any approach (`"*"`), beside the labeller's own factors, and the labeller's own stakes as its risks. Its approach is `novel`, its text the post (cut to 4,000 characters), and it has no cost.

A frame over 029's 32 factors is `too_many_factors`. The frame is checked against the obstacle as read (040's `check_attempt`, pure, nothing written), so a labelling that could never be framed is refused at once with the obstacle's reason. The turn records the obstacle on the labelling (`against`: `{obstacle, approach, novel}`, an optional key of the turn file, so turns written before it read as before). It is written with the running labelling, without an approach, and the reply's choice lands with the proposal. On read it is checked: an approach only once proposed or framed, a novel one named, ids as segments; anything else refuses the turn file.

**Confirming frames through the obstacle.** `confirm` runs the obstacle library's `check` (every check of its `frame`, the obstacle's and 029's, writing nothing), marks the turn framed, and then frames through the obstacle library, which links the attempt to the obstacle before 029 records the frame. A crash between the mark and the frame finishes on the next `confirm`, as before; a link written without its frame is framed again on that same attempt. An obstacle refusal at confirm (the obstacle was attempted or revised meanwhile) is handled like a 029 one: before the mark it leaves the labelling proposed (label again), and after the mark it unframes the labelling with that reason. Deciding is unchanged. A turn whose attempt at an obstacle is framed and undecided, and can still be decided, cannot be passed (`attempt_undecided`): decide only reaches the open turn, so a passed attempt would stay pending for good and its obstacle would take no other attempt. The director decides it, or the player confirms it, first. An attempt 029 cannot decide now (its actor changed hands since: `control_changed`), or a link whose 029 frame was never written (a confirm cut short), does pass, and is first withdrawn in the obstacle ledger (`ObstacleLibrary.withdraw`, a `withdrawal` entry, timed as `write.obstacle`): it is then `void` for good and brings nothing due, and it stays void when control comes back and 029 could decide it again, so the obstacle takes the next attempt. So is an attempt diverged by its own frame (a 029 revision made around the obstacle), which nothing could revise back once its turn had passed. The withdrawal is written before the passed turn: if it is refused, nothing passes. If the turn write fails after it, the turn stays open with its attempt withdrawn: `decide`, and `confirm`'s decision by rule, refuse an attempt at an obstacle that its ledger shows `void` (withdrawn or superseded) as `attempt_withdrawn`, checked under the lock a withdrawal takes, so a decision never crosses a withdrawal and such a turn can only be passed; a pass retried finds the attempt void already and only passes the turn. A fork choice (`choose`) withdraws the same way, before its marks are written, every undecided attempt of the branches it sets aside: nothing in play decides a set-aside turn, so its attempt would hold its obstacle pending for good. If a withdrawal is refused, nothing is chosen; if the choice is refused after it, choosing that branch again finds the attempts void. A set-aside branch chosen later after all (over a peer's conflicting marks) keeps its turn, and its withdrawn attempt is only passed.

**A linked attempt is framed against its obstacle only.** While the obstacle ledger links a turn's attempt and no later attempt superseded it (it is not `void`), labelling that turn plainly or against another obstacle is refused `obstacle_attempt_live` before any call is planned, and so is confirming a plain labelling of it (nothing is marked or framed): a plain 029 frame of a linked attempt carries none of the obstacle's facts and would leave the obstacle diverged for good. The player labels it against the obstacle again (a link whose 029 frame was refused for good is framed again on the same attempt), or plays on once another attempt has superseded it. Its approach is fixed by the link, and the labeller is told so: it is offered only that suggestion and asked to answer with it, or, for a novel approach, offered none and asked to answer `novel` listing exactly as many stakes as the link's (a novel approach risks the labelling's own stakes by id, `s1`...`sN`, so their number is part of what was linked; their words are the new labelling's). A reply that takes another approach, or a novel one with another number of stakes, is refused `approach_changed` and nothing is proposed, as confirming it would be.

**What comes due becomes the turn's effect.** A cost or a stake may carry the author's structured `changes` ([OBSTACLES](OBSTACLES.md#structured-changes)): at most two 017 relations that begin or end, or 023 holdings lines, with `actor` standing for whoever attempts it. Once the attempt is decided, `propose_effect` reads the obstacle's state and puts the changes of every consequence `due()` lists for that attempt (the stakes that came true, and on a success the approach's costs) into the proposal, as 021 operations and holdings lines with the acting character for `actor`, ahead of the proposer's own operations and lines. The acting character is the one 029 recorded attempting it (the decided frame's actor), never the turn file's word for it: a turn whose post names another actor (edited or synced since the confirm) is `obstacle_link_mismatch`, so no cost is redirected. The proposer's holdings note is kept. A change whose agent is the acting character is the player's voluntary act, backed by the post, so 021 names that player as an endorser. They go through the same keys as any effect: the director's approval, each endorser's, 029's `check_effect` and 021's preview. No model proposes them, and nothing is accepted without the keys. Refused: `not_linked` (the obstacle holds no attempt for the turn), `obstacle_diverged`, `undecided_attempt`, and the 021 or 023 reason of a change that cannot apply to this actor (a relation of the actor with themself is `self_relation`). An attempt a later one superseded (`void`) brings nothing due.

**The obstacle ledger says what a turn attempts.** Proposing and accepting read the obstacle ledger's own link for the turn's attempt (`ObstacleLibrary.linked`) and check it against the turn's `against`: a turn file that drops `against` while the ledger links its attempt, names another obstacle or approach, or claims an attempt the ledger never linked is `obstacle_link_mismatch` (written `refused` on the effect at acceptance). A linked attempt that is `void` makes nothing due, so a turn whose `against` is gone and whose attempt is void counts as attempting nothing, and takes a plain effect. So a hand edit cannot hide what an attempt made due by removing the turn's record of it.

**The director may waive a due change.** A due change can fail at acceptance (021's `precondition_failed` for a relation that already holds, 023's `insufficient` for a purse the actor cannot pay), fail when proposed (two consequences making the same change due are `conflicting_operations`, and the change is owed once for each, so an acceptance holds the effect to that count and one of them must be waived; a change backed by a post whose player no longer plays the character is refused by 021's authorization), or name the actor in a way 021 or 023 refuses. The director then proposes again with it waived: `propose_effect(..., waive=[Waiver("<change id>", "<reason>")])`. A due change's id is `<cost|stake>.<consequence id>.<n>`, its `n`th authored change (`cost.chit.2`); the reason is at most 200 characters. A waived change is left out of the proposal; a waiver that names no due change of the turn is `unknown_due_change`, and only the director's proposal waives (`not_director`; a turn file with a waiver on a player's proposal does not read). The waivers are kept on the effect (`waivers`, an optional key of the turn file, written only when there are some, sorted, tamper-checked on read) and are part of what is shown and keyed: the effect's `fingerprint` is its proposal's 021 fingerprint with each waiver's id and a digest of its reason, a player endorses that fingerprint (`fingerprint_mismatch` otherwise), and the authority records the endorsement over it, and 021 records the endorsement over its own fingerprint, as before. Every proposal is recorded by the authority too, by whoever proposes it, with or without waivers: a decision at the turn's address, the effect's number and its fingerprint, made under the store lock once the turn is read again and found unchanged (a proposal that would lose its turn write is refused `turn_changed` and records nothing), and before the turn records it. At acceptance a waiver counts only when the latest proposal recorded for the turn, of any effect number, is the director's own (the owner's trusted handle) as that effect over that exact fingerprint: the live effect is always the turn's last proposal. So neither a superseded proposal's record (copied over a later effect, or set live again in place), nor that of a proposal whose turn write failed after it recorded, covers a waiver once any later proposal of the turn is recorded, so a waiver edited into the turn file (with or without an endorsement, and on an effect no player endorses) counts for nothing: the change it would excuse is `due_missing`, and with nothing missing the effect is refused `invalid_waiver`. 030's recovery settles an acceptance cut short as before: its keys and waivers were checked before it started. An effect with no waiver is shown by its proposal's fingerprint, as before.

**An acceptance checks it is still there.** Before 021's preview, an effect of a turn framed against an obstacle must still hold every due operation and, first and in order, every due holdings line, but for those it waives. A turn file edited since, by hand or by a peer, that dropped one is written `refused` with `due_missing`. A runner without its obstacle library refuses the acceptance `no_obstacles` and writes nothing.

**Known limits.**
- **The obstacle is not secret.** 040 has no hidden facts: the frame the player confirms shows every authored fact that bears on the approach, with its bearing and its stakes. Secrets and a referee's view are later work.
- **A novel approach never escalates.** Its risks are the labeller's own stakes, never the obstacle's, so a failed novel attempt brings no escalation. This is the labeller-trust limit 040 already names. The labeller's own stakes carry no changes, so they make nothing due either.
- **Nothing proposes the effect by itself.** The director or the acting player calls `propose_effect` once the attempt is decided. A turn with nothing due is proposed as before.
- **Repeated changes are 021's to refuse.** Two consequences that make the same change due give an effect 021 refuses (`conflicting_operations`); the director waives one of them.
- **No obstacle library, no obstacle attempt.** A turn framed against an obstacle on a runner with no obstacle library (`obstacles=None`), whose attempt 029 could still decide, can be neither decided (`no_obstacles`) nor passed (`attempt_undecided`): it fails closed until the runner is given the library again.
- **A waiver is the effect's, not the obstacle's.** The obstacle's `due()` still lists a waived change as due: the waiver is recorded on the turn's effect only. A later effect of the turn (after a revocation) must waive it again.
- **A hand-over since the attempt blocks its actor's own changes.** A due change that is the acting character's own act is backed by the post, and once another player plays the character 021 no longer takes that backing: the effect is refused when proposed until the director waives that change.
- **The director's approval is not fingerprinted.** As for operations, the approval is the director's key on the turn as written when they give it. The waivers are pinned by the authority's record of their proposal (by effect number and fingerprint) and by the endorsements; finding the record scans the audit, as for an endorsement. So a superseded proposal copied with its waivers over a later effect the director approved without them is refused (`due_missing`), but an approval still says nothing of which content it was given for: an effect the director never saw, holding every due change, is approved as written.
- **A runner without its obstacle library reads no link.** A turn whose `against` is gone is then read as attempting nothing; the app configures the obstacle library with the adjudication key.
- **A change naming the acting character as one of the obstacle's actors** (the guard attempting his own door) may be refused by 021 or 023 (`self_relation`, `same_holder`) when proposed, never earlier; the director waives it.
- **Ledger failures read as 029's.** An obstacle ledger that cannot be read or trusted is reported as `adjudication_unreadable` or `adjudication_unavailable`, since the obstacle frames through 029.
- **One obstacle per action**, and one actor per attempt, as in 040.

## Accepting an effect

The effects part of 035 milestone 2: a turn's effect is proposed whole, kept on the turn, and accepted into [021](EFFECTS.md) once it holds every key, in a fixed order that [030](RECOVERY.md)'s recovery can finish after any crash.

**Proposing.** `propose_effect(chat, by, turn, summary, operations, holdings)` takes 021 `Op` values and an optional `EffectHoldings` (one 023 operation's lines; its attempt is the turn id). For an action framed against an obstacle, what the attempt made due is put first, but for what the director waives (`waive`, [Attempting an obstacle](#attempting-an-obstacle-040)). Nothing is proposed by a model in this slice, and ability use is refused (`not_in_this_slice`). The turn must be kept, or hold an action that is framed and decided (`not_kept`, `undecided_attempt`); a withheld action (or one on a private channel) takes none, as it is never labelled or framed (`private_action`). The owner proposes for any turn (`proposed_by="director"`), a player only for their own post's turn (`not_your_turn`, `proposed_by="player"`). The proposal takes the next campaign sequence (below), at the chat and the turn, and 021's `authorize` names who must endorse it: the controller of each player character whose voluntary act an operation or line is, backed by the turn's post (its id is the turn id). Only a human endorses (`non_human_controller`), and control that cannot be told is `ambiguous_control`; 021's own refusal reasons are passed on (`no_operations`, ...). The proposal is written `proposed` on the turn (`TurnRecord.effects`, numbered from 1, at most `MAX_EFFECTS` = 8), and a live earlier proposal of the turn is superseded. A turn with an effect being accepted or accepted already takes none (`effect_accepting`, `already_accepted`); one whose accepted effect was revoked may take another. An `accepted` effect is read in 021 first: one whose event was revoked through 021 since (`TurnEffectsLibrary.revoke`) is marked `revoked` in the same write as the new proposal.

**Forks, set-aside turns and sync conflicts** (028). Proposing, approving and endorsing are play: like a post or a keep, they are refused `turn_fork` while the chat is forked (until the owner chooses), `turn_conflict` while a turn of the chat is in a sync conflict, `paused` while any pause stands, and `unknown_turn` for a turn a choice set aside (it is no longer on the line). Each of their writes, and the `accepting` write below, is made only while no pause stands, no conflict is open and the turn is still on the line (`turn_changed` for a pause, a fork or a choice landing meanwhile).

**The keys.** The director (the library owner) approves (`approve_effect`, `not_director` for anyone else). Each named endorser endorses with their own trusted handle and the exact fingerprint they were shown (`endorse_effect`: `not_endorser`, `fingerprint_mismatch`), and only while the same players control the same characters (`control_changed`). Before the turn records it, the authority records the endorsement as a decision of that handle (`authority_unavailable` if it cannot); a recorded endorsement is presented to 021 again only with that record ([TOOL_AUTHORITY](TOOL_AUTHORITY.md)). A key goes to a live effect only (`not_proposed`). Whichever key comes last accepts the effect; until then it is written by compare-and-swap, so two keys given at once never both land on one read: the loser is refused `turn_changed` and gives it again. A new proposal has a new sequence, so a key given to an earlier one never fits it.

**Accepting**, in this order:
1. Nothing in the campaign waits for recovery (`recovery_needed`): no effect `accepting` on any turn, and no turn file that cannot be read (it could hide one). The refusal names this chat's unsettled turns and only counts the rest, so a player is never told another chat's turn ids.
2. 029's `check_effect`, the obstacle's due changes (`due_missing`, for an attempt at one), then 021's `accept` (a preview: nothing is written). A recorded endorsement the authority has no record of refuses `invalid_endorsement`, writing nothing. A refusal is written on the effect as `refused` with its reason (021's, such as `stale_sequence` or a failed precondition, or 029's), and the turn may be proposed for again. A store or ledger that is unavailable writes nothing (`effects_unavailable`, `adjudication_unavailable`); the keys wait.
3. Under the store lock, the chat is read again: play paused meanwhile (any pause of the chat: on its last turn, maybe a later one than the effect's, on a set-aside turn, or in another device's kept version) is `turn_changed`, and control changed since the call read it is `control_changed`. The effect is then written `accepting` with its keys, the event id 021 allocated, the sovereignty ledger position whose control it was authorized under (`controls_at`) and the tag of the device that started it (`accepted_on`: the library's device identity, as on its new turns; none on a library with no identity, and a damaged identity starts no acceptance, `device_identity`), by compare-and-swap, only while no pause stands, no sync conflict is open and the turn is still on the line: a turn changed since it was read (another key), a fork arriving or a choice that set the turn aside is `turn_changed`, and nothing is committed.
4. 021's commit. A library changed since the preview writes nothing: the effect goes back to `approved` (`effects_changed`), and the last key, given again, accepts it.
5. The effect is written `accepted`. This mark (and the put-back to `approved` in 4) records what 021 now holds over the runner's own `accepting` mark, so, like a finished take, it lands whatever a pause or a fork did to the chat meanwhile; only the turn file must be as written in 3.

**Recovery.** A crash after 3 leaves an `accepting` effect. `open` reports it as `("effect_unsettled", "<turn id>:<event id>:<shape>")`, with every 021 problem of the campaign as `("effects", "<code>:<event id>")`, and no other effect of the campaign is accepted until it is settled. `recover_effects(chat, by)` (the owner only) settles the chat's, oldest first and then those on turns a fork choice set aside (an acceptance cut short on a turn set aside later still blocks the campaign, and is still reported and settled), by the shape it finds. It only publishes or marks what an acceptance already committed, so it goes on while play waits: over a pause, and while the chat is forked (it writes only the effect's own turn, on whichever branch). It needs a readable device identity (`device_identity`), and settles only this library's acceptances (below). It still needs every turn file of the chat readable (`turns_unreadable`), and waits while a turn of the chat is in a sync conflict (`turn_conflict`, checked again under the store lock before each settlement): each device then reads its own version of the turn, and a `resolve` could undo the mark. A proposed or approved effect on a set-aside turn blocks nothing and takes no key.

**An acceptance another device started.** Sync carries turns but not 021's accepted events, relations or holdings ([SYNC](SYNC.md)), so a turn can arrive whose effect is `accepting` with 021's files of it only on the device that started it: read here it would look `unpublished`, and settling it here would put back or drop an effect that may have landed there. So an `accepting` effect is this device's when its `accepted_on` is this device's tag (or both are none, a library never identified), and, whatever its tag, whenever this library's 021 holds any trace of its event (any shape but `unpublished`): an identity replaced since (`identify(replace=True)`, a copied library's fix) or lost never strands what landed here, which still blocks the campaign and is settled as below. 021's event ids are random, so another device's event never shares an id with one here. Otherwise nothing of it is here, and it is never settled on a guess: one tagged with another device is reported as `("effect_elsewhere", "<turn id>:<event id>:<device tag>")`, and `recover_effects` refuses it `accepted_elsewhere` (that device settles it, and sync carries its mark here); one that names no device on an identified library (an older record of this library's, or an acceptance from a device without identity) is reported as `("effect_untagged", "<turn id>:<event id>")`, and `recover_effects` refuses it `untagged_acceptance` unless the owner says it is this library's (`own=True`: it is then put back to `approved`, or dropped when aborting). Either kind blocks none of this device's acceptances (no 021 file of it is here to meet) and is skipped while the chat's others are settled; the refusal comes after, counting those settled first and naming at most two of the rest. Its turn still takes no new proposal (`effect_accepting`), nor once it is `accepted` on another device with nothing of it here (`accepted_elsewhere`). An acceptance this device started and cut short before anything landed, and then read under a new or lost identity, is indistinguishable from another device's and waits the same way. With a damaged identity whose each one is cannot be told: `open` reports them all as `effect_unsettled`, and recovery and acceptance refuse `device_identity`. The shapes of this device's own:
- `unpublished` (nothing landed): back to `approved`, keys kept; approving again accepts it;
- `landed` (the event is in force and its receipts verify): `accepted`;
- `complete` (the event landed without some of its changes: 030's shape A): 030's `complete`, then `accepted`;
- `adopt` (changes landed without their event file: shape B): 030's `adopt`, checked under the control recorded with `accepting` (replayed from the sovereignty ledger at `controls_at`), so a character that changed hands since never strands it, and on each recorded endorsement, which needs the authority's record of it or that endorser present now with their own trusted handle (`endorsers`); then `accepted`;
- `revoked` (revoked since): `revoked`;
- `unreadable` (a file that may hold part of it cannot be read): refused `effects_unreadable` until it is repaired.

`abort=True` undoes instead: an unpublished effect is `refused` (`aborted`); one that landed is revoked with 021's `revoke(partial=True)` (adopted first for shape B), with every endorser present now with their own trusted handle (`endorsers`; `not_endorsed`, `bad_handle`), and is `revoked` (`aborted`).

**Sequences.** A labelling and an effect proposal take one campaign sequence: one after the newest knowledge sequence, every accepted event's (active or revoked) and every labelling's and proposal's on the campaign's turns, read raw, under the store lock with the write that uses it. So a campaign's sequences only increase, as 021's ledger asks.

**Who sees it.** A proposal lives on its turn file only: it never enters what any listener heard, a brief, the labeller's window or the table's lines. An accepted effect is 021 truth, read through 021 like any other.

**Refusals**: `no_effects`, `bad_effects`, `not_in_this_slice`, `private_action`, `not_kept`, `undecided_attempt`, `not_your_turn`, `unknown_turn`, `unknown_effect`, `too_many_effects`, `effect_accepting`, `already_accepted`, `not_proposed`, `not_director`, `not_endorser`, `fingerprint_mismatch`, `control_changed`, `non_human_controller`, `ambiguous_control`, `authority_unavailable` (an endorsement the authority could not record), `invalid_endorsement` (a recorded endorsement the authority has no record of), `recovery_needed`, `no_adjudication`, `effects_changed`, `effect_unsettled` (cut short: recover it), `accepted_elsewhere` (another device's acceptance: settle it there), `untagged_acceptance` (no device named and nothing of it here: `own=True` if it is this library's), `device_identity`, `effects_unavailable`, `effects_unreadable`, `not_endorsed`, `turn_changed`, `turn_fork`, `turn_conflict`, `turns_unreadable`, `paused`, `no_obstacles`, `not_linked`, `obstacle_diverged`, `obstacle_link_mismatch`, `due_missing`, `unknown_due_change`, `invalid_waiver`, and 021's reasons, passed on.

**Known limits.**
- **Structured proposals only.** No model proposes an effect from kept narration yet, and nothing checks the narration against the effect; that is this milestone's later part and the Narration Continuity Referee's (047). No ability use is proposed or charged.
- **A recorded key is presented again.** When the director's approval is the last key, or after a restart (`adopt`), the runner presents each recorded endorsement to 021 with a handle it mints for that player from the library's authority (TOOL_AUTHORITY's 035 exception), only when the authority's `security` audit holds the decision that player's own trusted handle made when endorsing. An endorsement written into a turn file by hand or by a peer is refused `invalid_endorsement`; the player endorses again. The audit is as trusted as the authority ledger (its tail can be forged with local file access, as can every grant). An endorsement recorded on a turn by a build before this check has no such record and is refused the same way. Finding it scans the audit, which grows with every decision.
- **The summary is shown, not checked.** A proposal is never changed on its turn, so the summary a player saw beside the operations is the one accepted (a new wording is a new proposal, with a new sequence and fingerprint). Nothing checks that it stays neutral director description of what the operations say (021's obligation); showing it is the UI's, which does not exist yet.
- **An older proposal behind a newer acceptance** is refused `stale_sequence` (021's sequence floor): propose it again to give it a new sequence.
- **One holdings attempt per turn.** A proposal with holdings uses the turn id as its 021 attempt, so once a holdings effect of the turn was revoked (or aborted) leaving its holdings change in force, another one for that turn is refused `attempt_revoked` until that is repaired.
- **One process.** The sequence is taken under this process's store lock; two processes on one library can take one sequence twice (021 allows equal sequences).
- **Accepted effects do not sync, though turns do.** 021's accepted events, the relations of a campaign that holds one and 023's holdings are still held back by sync ([SYNC](SYNC.md)), so each device has its own 021 stack while turns, with the effects recorded on them, travel. Two devices playing one chat offline can each accept an effect on their own branch's turn into their own 021 (each a valid stack there); merging those stacks, and choosing a branch whose effect the other device accepted, is 028's merge milestone. An `accepting` effect from another device is left to that device ([above](#accepting-an-effect)); an `accepted` one tagged with another device, with nothing of it in this library's 021, gives its turn no other effect here (`accepted_elsewhere`); an untagged one is read in 021 as before (021's `effects_unavailable` when it is not here). Neither device's sequences see the other's, so the two stacks can hold the same sequence.
- **The recovery gate is campaign-wide.** Any `accepting` effect or any unreadable turn file of the campaign blocks every acceptance in it, and `recover_effects` settles only the chat it is asked for.
- **A receipt mismatch the runner did not leave blocks nothing.** Every acceptance this runner left partly published has an `accepting` effect, which the gate covers. Any other mismatch (a 017 `retract` or `reopen` of an accepted relation, a restore or a copy that lost a file) is not the runner's to settle, and 021 accepts unrelated effects over it, so the runner does too; `open` keeps reporting it as `("effects", "receipt_mismatch:<event id>")`. The owner repairs it through 021 and 030 directly: `revoke(partial=True)` (with every endorser's own handle), or 030's `complete` for a latest event that lost a file, before accepting another effect (`complete` finishes only the latest event, `not_latest` otherwise). The runner offers neither yet.
- **A revocation through 021 is seen on the next proposal.** The turn reads `accepted` until a new `propose_effect` on it reads 021 and marks it `revoked`; `open` does not.
- **An abort needs every endorser present.** A player who has left the table blocks an abort of a landed effect; recovering it as accepted needs only the director, even after a character changed hands (an `accepting` effect recorded before `controls_at` existed is adopted under today's control, and may then be refused).
- **Shape B without the endorsement's audit record.** When the library's `authority/` lacks the endorsement's record (a ledger restored from before it, or another device the turn and its landed relations reached: `authority/` never syncs), the director alone cannot adopt it (`invalid_endorsement`): recovering it as accepted, or aborting it, then needs each such endorser present with their own trusted handle (`endorsers`). Until then the effect stays `accepting` and the campaign's gate stays shut; a player who never returns leaves it to a human repair.
- **Unreadable holder files** anywhere in the campaign make every unsettled shape `unreadable`, and stop its recovery, until repaired.

## Pause, skip and redirect (016)

016's mid-session requests (a [session zero](SESSION_ZERO.md#mid-session-requests) request needs no configuration) are run two ways that take one path: `request(chat, by, kind, targets, *, direction=None)`, the click, and typed text that the [intent router](intent-routing.md#commands-and-gates) reads as one of the table's commands, which `post` runs instead of saving a post.

**Typed requests** (the requester's standing "make reasonable choices", decided in Wave 6):
- **Lanes.** `skip` ("skip", "skip that/this/it") and `redirect` ("redirect", "redirect that", or "redirect: <direction>": only a `:` introduces the player's own words, so "Redirect, you fool!" reads as command-like and is refused `uncertain`, as "Stop, you fool!" is, and so does a direction with no word in it, "redirect: ..."; the direction is the text after the colon, as typed, less leading colons and trailing whitespace and `.!?`) are catalog commands with the `authorized` gate, beside `pause` and `resume`. A post whose text routes to `pause`, `resume`, `skip` or `redirect` runs as `request` (or `resume`) would, for the same authenticated player, and returns the turn it wrote; no post is saved. Every other command is still `not_in_this_slice`, a typed `reroll` included (the app calls `generate`, so `post` keeps returning a turn). An uncertain reading ("Skip the formalities, Mara", "could you skip that") is refused `uncertain` with nothing done; quoting, or speech or action mode, keeps the words in the fiction.
- **Not a safe stop.** A skip or redirect changes what the table sees, so unlike `pause` it is never acted on below the voice confidence threshold or when an alternative transcript differs: it is offered as a candidate, and said or clicked again.
- **What a typed skip or redirect is about: the lines on show.** On the chat's last turn, while it is open, its newest take by number, and only while that take is still proposed; once the turn is closed, its kept take. Of that take, only the shown lines the requester heard (below; the owner, every shown line). It never walks back to an older take or turn, so a retried "skip" (a double Enter) withdraws nothing more: with nothing left to target (a new post with nothing generated, a newest take already withdrawn, a kept take already skipped) it is refused `no_targets` and nothing is written. Naming other lines is `request`'s.
- **Authority and record, as for a pause.** The requester is `_principal`'s: a trusted human handle this library's authority issued, for a player of a present character or the owner (`not_authenticated`, `not_at_table`). **A player withdraws only what their own character heard**: a typed request targets only the lines whose audience holds a character that player controls (003, as recorded now), and a `skip`, `redirect` or `rephrase` naming any other line is refused `not_heard`, as is a `strike` of one (the review's call in Wave 6: a line behind a shut door is not theirs to take away, and would carry their name). On a take played on a [stage](#the-stage-041) only the audience's player characters as of that take count (`Take.players`, as the director's view reads them; a staged take that recorded none has no listener, fail closed), so an NPC handed to a player later brings none of what it heard; an unstaged take counts its whole audience. Those characters are then matched to players by control now. The owner may withdraw or strike any line. A pause records `paused_by` (for `resume` only); a withdrawn line records `by`, and a redirect its direction on the take. A typed skip, like a click, goes on while play is paused, and waits for nothing but a readable, unforked, unconflicted chat (`turns_unreadable`, `turn_fork`, `turn_conflict`).

**The authenticated audience.** 016's `BoundaryContext.audience` is no longer the requester alone: it is every human that 003's sovereignty ledger records as controlling a present player character (the table, on stage or not), plus the requester. It is read from the ledger on every call, never from a post, a reply or the caller. With one local player it is that player (and the owner, when the owner asks). Who among several humans may decide or confirm is still [102](roadmap/sprints/collaborative-multiplayer.md)'s.

**A skip never erases or replaces accepted canon.** A kept take's lines on a turn whose 021 effect is `accepted` (or `accepting`: fail closed) depict that canon, so `segments` gives them as 016 `accepted` segments naming the effect's event; every other shown line (a proposed take, a kept take with no accepted effect) stays a `proposed` segment.
- A **skip** of an accepted segment passes over its depiction (016's `skip_presentation`): the line is withdrawn, so nobody hears it from then on, no NPC's window or the director's view holds it, and future proposed narration is built without it. The turn's effect, 021's event and its relations and holdings, and 029's outcome of the action stay exactly as accepted; a skip writes only the turn's lines, never an effect.
- A **redirect** leaves an accepted segment alone (016) and withdraws only the proposals it names; one that would withdraw nothing is refused `accepted_canon`, and nothing is written. A **rephrase** withdraws the line as before. On an open turn its new proposal for the same canon comes from `generate`; on a kept turn nothing regenerates it (`generate` opens a new turn), so a rephrase of a kept line over an accepted effect works as a permanent skip of that depiction: the effect, its event and the outcome stay, and the line is gone from what is shown.
- A **reroll** (`generate` again on the open turn of a decided action whose effect is accepted) needs the same authority as any play, is a fresh proposal kept only through `keep`, never hears a skipped or withdrawn take, and leaves the effect and the outcome as they are.

**Turn files.** Nothing new is written: a skip is the same `withdrawn` mark, so every turn written before reads and writes as before.

**Limits.**
- No theme is labelled on a line yet, so a skip is always the player's own request; a session zero's `skip` treatment is not applied automatically (a scope with one still refuses generation, below).
- A skipped line is gone from what is shown, with no placeholder; showing "skipped" and the accepted outcome in its place is the UI's (065).
- **A player whose character changed hands loses its lines.** Who heard a line is matched to players by control now, not as of the line (no ledger position is recorded): once Anya goes to the director, her former player can no longer skip or strike what Anya heard, and a player given a character that was a player character when it heard a line may. The owner can still withdraw or strike it.
- A withdrawn line cannot be brought back; ask the cast again. A kept turn is never reopened, so a rephrase there (canon's depiction included) is never re-rendered on its turn: it ends as a skip.
- `by` on a withdrawn line names the requester in the turn file. 016's decision never echoes them, but whoever reads the file can; hiding it from other players is the UI's and 102's.
- The direction is recorded, not sent to the model (below).

## Storage and sync

- `turns/` is a history family: `load()` never reads it, and `store.campaign_turns(campaign)` lists it with per-file problems. A turn is created once (create-only, so two posts can never both become turn *n*: a library never creates turn *n* while its line holds a turn *n*, under any tag, and a new turn follows the line's last turn as read) and then changed only by compare-and-swap. A chat's turns are ordered by number, then an untagged turn first, then by device tag: one order on every device.
- A turn file of this chat that cannot be read stops every write to the chat, marks and pauses included (`turns_unreadable`), as does a turn whose predecessor is missing or cannot be told (`turn_gap`); a fork (`turn_fork`, every file readable) stops them too, refused `turn_fork`. A damaged turn of another chat does not. A turn of this chat that sync left in a keep-both conflict that has not ended (019: the devices each read their own version) stops every write to the chat but a pause (`turn_conflict`; `open` reports `("turn_conflict", <turns>)`): a post, a run, a labelling, a keep, a pass, a choice, a resume, a strike, and the marks and withdrawals of a `skip`, `redirect` or `rephrase` request are all refused; a pause in the other device's version kept beside a turn holds play as well (`ChatTurns.kept_pauses`). The conflict is found whatever its base (a device that lost its archive), whether or not the peer holds a version to keep (a turn one device deleted is a `deleted` conflict: sync never propagates a turn's deletion), and whichever pair's keep first wrote the peer's bytes here (`open` in the pair's `keeps.json`, [SYNC](SYNC.md#played-turns-035-028s-turn-merge)). It ends with `resolve`, with a sync that finds the devices agreeing, on this device alone when the owner dismisses the peer's version kept here (`grimoire.sync.dismiss`, 019's local "keep mine": the next sync with that peer still reports the conflict), or when the owner ends the pairing with that peer (`unpair`, also for a lost peer, or one never paired that 019 synced directly): the records of an unpaired peer hold no chat. A kept-version record of a peer still paired that cannot be honoured (damaged, a newer format, unreadable, or lost: a completed sync always leaves one) holds every chat of a campaign that pair may have synced (its archive names a file of it, or cannot be read) or one with kept versions of its turns here, as `<sync records unreadable>`, until the next sync with that peer replaces it or the owner unpairs it. A library whose device identity is damaged or missing while it holds pair records holds every chat the same way, until `identify` gives it an identity again; a new identity keeps every conflict that stands. A `resolve` that is refused or fails before its data lands ends nothing.
- Every actor id a turn names stays taken for 107's allocators (`turn_instance_ids`), set-aside turns included, so a deleted actor's id never goes to a newcomer who would inherit what it heard.
- **Sync carries turns** ([SYNC](SYNC.md#what-is-synchronized)): a turn is a mutable record, so it follows the ordinary rows (copied over the agreed version, keep-both on concurrent edits), and it never lands without its chat record, the play scope, the turn it follows, the turn its mark names, the actors it names that the other device holds and the revisions its lines pin. Two devices playing one chat offline both write its next turn, as two files with two ids, and the next sync brings both to each device: the chat is then [forked](#forked-chats).

### Forked chats

<a id="forked-chats"></a>028's turn merge (`grimoire.scene.branches`, `SceneLibrary.choose`, `SceneRunner.choose`). Every turn names the turn it was played after, so a chat's turns are a tree, and its **line** is read from turn 1: while one turn follows the line's last turn, the line goes on. When two or more do, the chat is **forked** there:
- `open` reports it twice: `("turns", "<chat>:turn_fork")`, as before, and `("turn_fork", <detail>)` naming the fork's number and each branch by its first turn's id, its device tag (`untagged` for none), how many turns it holds from that number on, `this device` for this library's own, and `set aside` for one a choice set aside. `Scene.turns.fork` holds the same as data (`Fork`: `number`, `after`, `state`, `branches`; a `Branch`: `head`, `device`, `turns`, `set_aside`), for a UI to offer the choice. The scene shows every branch not set aside, in the one order, exactly as before 028's merge, and every write is refused `turn_fork`.
- **Nothing is chosen automatically.** The library owner calls `choose(chat, by, turn)` with the first turn of one branch (a player of a present character may not: choosing decides what everyone at the table heard). In one commit, by compare-and-swap on each file, every other branch head gets a `set_aside` mark naming the chosen head, and a mark on the chosen head is cleared. The marked heads and **every turn played after them** are set aside: kept on disk as they were, heard by no one, in no NPC's window and not in the table's view, taking no number in the line and no part in the fork check, with the actor ids they name still taken. A set-aside line can neither be struck nor withdrawn (`unknown_line`). Play goes on from the chosen branch. A choice writes only those marks, never a turn of the chosen branch. A pause on a turn it sets aside still holds the chat where it was made (see `resume`), and a paused turn arriving later and set aside by its `follows` pauses it too. The choice is made only while every turn of the chat is as it was read (`turn_changed` otherwise: a pause or a mark landing meanwhile, anywhere in the chat).
- **One mark per losing branch, on the turn it governs.** Two devices making the same choice write the same bytes, so they converge. A turn arriving later from a third device that was played after a set-aside turn is set aside too, by its `follows`. A new branch arriving after a choice (a third device's own turn *n*) makes the chat forked again, with the set-aside branch kept aside: choose again.
- **Marks that choose nothing are a conflict, never a guess.** Unless exactly one branch is unmarked and every mark names it (or, with more than one unmarked branch, every mark names one of them), the fork's state is `conflict` (`choices conflict` in the detail): no mark counts, every branch is shown, writes are refused `turn_fork`, and the owner chooses again. Two devices choosing differently offline each mark the other's branch: sync copies both marks (two files), so both devices show the same conflict, and neither silently flips to the other's choice. When both devices rewrote the same head (three branches, say), sync reports an ordinary keep-both conflict on that file, and each device reads its own marks until it is resolved.
- A fork already settled is never chosen again (`not_forked`). A later fork of the chosen branch is a fork again. A choice made further down a branch still holds when an earlier fork opens again (a third device's turn arriving late): that branch is shown without what the later choice set aside. The report names the chat's first fork only: a fork, or a conflict of choices, further down a branch is reported once the line reaches it.
- **A number played again.** A device that chose another device's branch plays the numbers its own set-aside turns hold again, so its own `<chat>.<n>-<tag>` id is taken: such a turn takes the first free **replay tag** instead, the first eight hex digits of the SHA-256 of `<tag>.<k>` (k = 1, 2, ...). A library with no identity has nothing unique to derive one from, so it refuses such a turn `device_identity`: give it an identity, then play on. A replayed turn's chat id is at most 88 characters, as for any tagged turn.

## The stage (041)

<a id="the-stage-041"></a>[041](STAGING.md)'s stage in play (035 milestone 2, 041's obligations on this runner). **Nothing persists a stage yet**: that is 041 milestone 2's store. Until it exists the app keeps its stages and gives the runner a `StageSource`; `StageBook` is the smallest one, one process's latest stage of each chat in memory (`put(chat, stage)`; `latest(chat)`), lost when the process ends. The runner never changes a stage: who moves, opens a door or leaves is the app's change to make with 041's `apply`, and nothing turns narration or a post into a `Change` yet.

**Read and checked every call, fail closed.** Every call reads the chat's latest stage from the source, beside the cast 033 stages now, and checks it before anything is played (`Scene.stage`; `Scene.on_stage` is the cast less the stage's absences). A stage that does not hold is not used, `open` reports it as `("stage", <reason>)`, and `begin`, `post`, `generate`, `label`, `confirm`, `decide` and `keep` are refused; nothing falls back to the cast alone, since a whisper would then pass a shut door:
- the source fails or gives something that is not a `Stage` (`stage_unreadable`), or gives none for a chat played on a stage (`stage_lost`): one this runner saw a stage for, or any of whose recorded takes holds an on-stage cast (`Take.on_stage`), so a restart, or a runner with no source at all, is no way back to the cast alone;
- the stage is older than the latest this runner played the chat with (`stale_stage`), or another stage at the same sequence (`stage_diverged`): a stage has no lineage, so the runner keeps the latest one it saw of each chat and refuses one that goes back. `StageBook.put` refuses the same two, and a stage of another chat (`scene_mismatch`);
- `stage_mismatch` for every other refusal: the stage is of another campaign or scene (a chat's stage has the chat id as its scene id: `scene_mismatch`), no cast could be staged (`cast_unstaged`), or 041's `check_cast` refuses it against the cast as it is now: its actors are not exactly the cast (`cast_mismatch`: a card attached or detached, a member boxed or reserved since the stage was made) or the cast no longer holds (`stale_cast`). A recast is a new `Stage` of the cast as it is, at a later sequence.

The stage is judged as the scene now, as the cast is (033's `NOW`, after every recorded moment): `check_cast` gets `stage.at(NOW)`, the source's word that nothing physical changed since its stage's own sequence. `Scene.stage` is the stage as the source gave it, at its own sequence.

**Who hears, sees and speaks, with a stage:**
- **A post's audience** is 032's `transmit` over 041's `presence` for the message (the sender and those who physically receive it on its own channel), never the cast alone. A whisper reaches only its addressee in the same area, so a whisper through a shut door, or through an open one, is refused `addressee_absent` and nothing is written. Room speech stops at a shut door (with nobody in earshot, the speaker alone hears it) and carries through an open one. Speech heard through an open way is in the audience like any other: 041's `deliver` would make it `overheard`, but no 020 delivery is written in this slice, so the distinction is not recorded.
- **A visible action** is seen by its actor and those who can see it by 041's sight rule (not through a shut door, not inside a thing), not by everyone present. A withheld one is still its sender's alone.
- **An NPC line** (and the opening greeting) is heard by its speaker and every actor in earshot of it: its own area and the next through an open way. Each speaker's audience is judged before any call, so a take that would rest on a guess bills nothing. A narrator line is still the table's (the present player characters, on stage or not): narration is not heard in the room.
- **Within a take, a later call is given only the earlier lines its actor heard.** 027's `run_turn` takes a hearing rule (`hears`), and with a stage the runner passes one: a later NPC speaker is given an earlier line (in its prompt, and as what its reply is screened against) only when it is in that line's audience, so a line never crosses a shut door through the take itself; the director is given only the lines some present player character heard; a narrator line is never given to an NPC. Without a stage every call hears every earlier line, as before.
- **The director sees only what the table heard, across turns as well, judged as of each line's turn.** It narrates to the table, so its brief's `heard` and its lore's `table_text` (`table_lines(..., as_heard=True)`) hold a kept line of a staged take only when a player character as of that take is in its audience: each take played on a stage records the player characters by control as it is written (`Take.players`, the turn payload's optional `players`, written only beside `on_stage`; checked against its narration's audience on read, and against control now by `keep`, which refuses `cast_changed` if who plays whom changed since). Control changing later changes nothing: a line no player character heard stays out after its listener is handed to a player, and a line a player character heard stays in after that character is handed to the director. An NPC's line behind a shut door that no player character heard so never reaches the director, in its take or a later one. A staged take that recorded no `players` (an earlier build's) gives the director none of its lines (fail closed). A whisper between a player character and an NPC includes that character, so it stays; posts (typed at the table, never private ones) stay. A take played with no stage gives every kept line, so an unstaged chat's director brief is as before.
- **An absent cast member does not speak in the room.** A cast member the stage has off stage is not a default speaker; naming one is refused `not_on_stage`; an off-stage persona's post is refused `not_on_stage`; the launch character's greeting is not spoken while it is off stage (an explicitly chosen one is refused `not_on_stage`; otherwise the chat opens from its premise, or `no_opening`).
- **Never on a guess.** Who hears or sees a line decides what each actor later knows, so a line whose audience rests on assumed staging is refused `rests_on_assumption`, as 041's `presence` refuses: for speech and visible actions, every actor's staging counts; for a whisper only the addressees'. Establish the staging (a `place` or `remove` with the default basis) first.
- **Keeping** re-judges every line's audience on the current stage, and the take's on-stage cast against the current one: a take written before someone left, or a door shut, is refused `cast_changed`, and the table generates again. It re-judges sight too: each intention records whom its NPC perceived when it chose (`Intention.perceived`, the turn payload's optional `perceived`, written only on a stage), and a take is refused `cast_changed` when any of its agenda NPCs now perceives someone else (someone stepped out of the wardrobe, or into it), since 039's choice was judged against who it could see.
- **Who is present to an NPC.** With a stage, the turn's plan, 039's choice (the cast it splits into recognised and unrecognised) and the director's brief take the on-stage cast (`Scene.on_stage`), and the take records it (`Take.on_stage`, the turn payload's optional `on_stage`, written only on a take played on a stage), so an actor who has left is never `Present` to a choice and a reload can still check each intention against the take's scene. Of that cast, 039's conditions see only whom the NPC perceives: an actor on stage it cannot see (behind a shut door, hidden inside a thing) is `unrecognised` to its choice, which no condition sees, so a `Present` of them is unmet and a limit watching for them does not apply; the intention's scene (present and unrecognised together) is still the take's on-stage cast. Each NPC's pack (and a labelled player character's) takes the cast that actor perceives as `present` (031's contract): itself and whoever it can see by 041's sight rule, so someone behind a shut door ranks none of its memory as present. An older build refuses a take with `on_stage` as an unknown field, never dropping it.

**Sequences: one per staged step.** 041's `apply` takes one change per campaign sequence and judges `deliver` and `perceptions` on the stage at their record's own sequence; 021 accepts one effect per turn at one sequence. The decision (with 041 milestone 2): **a turn takes one campaign sequence for each staged step**, a message that must be judged between steps (a whisper before a leave) included, allocated in order by the same allocator as labellings and effect proposals ([Sequences](#accepting-an-effect): one after every sequence already used), and the turn's single 021 effect, if it has one, takes a sequence after its last staged step, so its durable consequences, and the turn's event's witnesses, are judged on the stage after every step of the turn. So 041's one-change-per-sequence rule and 021's one-effect-per-turn rule both stand, and the states between steps stay ordered. The alternative, an ordered batch of changes at one sequence, would need `deliver` and `perceptions` to say which point of the batch they judge against; it is not taken. Nothing of this is wired yet: the runner judges the source's latest stage now, records no staged step on a turn, and the allocator counts none. When 041 milestone 2 records staged steps, it must record each step's sequence on the turn (or its own family) so the allocator counts them.

**Known limits.**
- No stage is persisted, and the runner's memory of the latest stage of each chat lasts as long as the runner. A chat with a take recorded on a stage is never played by the cast alone again (`stage_lost`, also after a restart), but one whose stage was given and lost before any take recorded it (only `post`s since, or no turn yet) still is after a restart, and a source that hands back an older stage after a restart is not caught. 041 milestone 2's store is the fix (a recorded stage, so "no stage" is itself recorded). The memory is not locked: two threads of one runner reading two different stages at once can both pass.
- An NPC's choice is made on sight alone: someone it can hear but not see (hidden inside a thing in its own room, or itself inside one) is not `Present` to it either, and its brief and the director's still list the whole on-stage cast as the scene, so the model is told who is on stage, not who this NPC perceives. A per-NPC scene would change 027's `intention_cast_mismatch` and 047's rule together.
- An off-stage player character's player still acts at the table (keep, pass, pause, label an earlier action); only posting needs the character on stage.
- No telepath is passed to 041's `presence` (as before: telepathy is refused `not_telepathic` until 025 says who may send it).
- Speech through an open way is not marked `overheard` on the turn (see above), and no 012 perception is written for a visible action.

## Known limits

- **Merged turns.** While a turn is in a keep-both conflict each device reads its own version, and play waits (`turn_conflict`) until it is resolved; how it is resolved (which version is kept, a pause included) is the person's call in `resolve` ([SYNC](SYNC.md#played-turns-035-028s-turn-merge)): resolving to the version without the pause drops the other device's pause, as dismissing the paused version kept here lifts its hold on this device. A turn whose predecessor was in such a conflict stays after the version that won. A version that cannot be dismissed (its conflict has no known base, or its bytes were first kept here by another pair) holds the chat until a sync, a `resolve` or `unpair`.
- **The cost of a read.** Each read of a chat's turns arranges them all and reads, for every peer of this device, its pairing record and its kept-version record (and its archive, when that record cannot be honoured), plus the kept versions of the chat's turns in conflict; a write reads them again under the store lock. The cost grows with the chat, the number of peers and the sync history; nothing is cached between reads.
- **One turn file per write.** A post, a take or a mark is one compare-and-swap; a concurrent writer is refused `turn_changed`. A new turn is created only while the previous turn is unchanged since it was read and no turn of the chat holds an open pause, so a pause written meanwhile (on this device or arriving by sync) is never played over; a keep, a pass, a reroll and the opening's take are refused `turn_changed` under the lock too while any pause stands, and so is one whose turn a fork choice set aside since it was read (a run never starts on a turn no one hears). A run already in flight still lands its take over its own running take, with every call's attempts and usage, whatever a pause, a fork, a choice or a sync conflict did to its chat meanwhile; the take then waits for the resume, the choice or the resolve. When a `resolve` replaced its turn with the other device's version meanwhile, that version stands as the owner chose it, and the run's take joins it usage-only (`stale`, no lines) under the number its calls were billed under, so the next sync carries the usage to the other device; only when that version holds its own take of that number (both devices ran it offline) is the usage left unrecorded, refused `turn_changed`. A labelling in flight does the same, as a `superseded` labelling. A rewrite keeps the envelope's owner and unknown fields. A run that loses that race is written again as a usage-only stale take, up to three times.
- **A reply to a whisper is spoken aloud.** An NPC answering a whisper speaks to the room, so if the model repeats what was whispered, everyone present hears it. Private NPC replies are 035 M2.
- **No control history.** `seat` records control for the chat's current actors; reassigning a character mid-chat is 003's.
- **Party.** The cast is 033's staged `SceneCast` (`Scene.staged`; `Scene.cast` is its `present`), staged with 033's `stage` under the chat id as the scene id, from the chat's active bound actors less 033's reserved members (stored away: left out, not a problem) and members whose membership is uncertain (left out and reported `party_uncertain`, fail closed). A member `stage` refuses is left out and reported by its reason: `missing_actor` (no instance file) or `instance_id_conflict` (its id is also used by another kind). While a relation file cannot be read no cast is staged (`cast_unstaged`, `relations_unreadable`): nobody is present, so no player plays a present character. The library owner is refused `party_unreadable` by `begin`, `post` and `generate` and `cast_changed` by `keep`; a player who is not the owner is refused `not_at_table` before that. Any party problem refuses generation. The cast is staged afresh on every call, never stored; with a stage, 041's `check_cast` checks it against `Scene.staged` on every call ([The stage](#the-stage-041)).
- **Budgets are per take, not per chat.** What bounds a turn's model spend is: the inference policy's per-turn call caps for its optional stages (a director reply needs `director_generation`) and one baseline call per speaking NPC, as 027 plans them; `max_attempts_per_call` retries per call; the runtime's `max_output_tokens` per attempt (027's default 400); the context `Budget` for each pack and the director's lore; and at most 16 takes per turn (`MAX_TAKES`). The caps apply to one take's plan (`<turn_id>.<take>`), so every reroll gets a fresh allowance: a turn can spend up to 16 times one take's caps. There is **no running spend cap** across takes, turns or chats, and usage is recorded in tokens only, with **no money cost**. A running spend cap and priced usage belong to [Turn Costs And Feature Flags](roadmap/sprints/turn-costs-and-feature-flags.md) (004) and [Model Routing And Budgeting](roadmap/sprints/model-routing-and-budgeting.md) (013).
- **History cost still grows with the chat.** Every runner call loads the library afresh (`store.load()`), reads and hashes every turn file of the chat (another chat's are skipped by name, and a file is decoded again only when its bytes changed), and reads the chat's imported transcript pages; a write reads the chat's turns again under the lock. So a call's cost still grows roughly linearly with the chat's played history, though a warm call at 500 turns is two to three times faster than before 048: see [PERFORMANCE](PERFORMANCE.md) for the synthetic numbers. A per-chat index that avoids reading every file, and caching the library view, are later work of [Performance And Tracing](roadmap/sprints/performance-and-tracing.md).
- **No `last_activity` update** per post (107's chat record would need a second write in the commit).
- **Imported history.** Every call decodes every page of the chat (a long SillyTavern chat costs a fraction of a second per call; see *History cost*), though only the last `MAX_HEARD` lines are used. It grants no knowledge: no 020 delivery is written for an imported line, as for a played one. Unreadable pages blank the history but stop no write, unlike an unreadable turn: an NPC answering without it misses lines, never hears one it should not, and once the pages read again the history counts as heard before turn 1. 026's V0.2.1 pages record no page count, so a lost last page of theirs goes unnoticed. 026 writes the pages of a chat it imported before 036 on a later run, possibly after the chat was played: the history then sits before turns already kept, and `begin`'s refusal could not apply. A SillyTavern import with no speaker mapped has no imported cast, so nobody hears its history. A V0.2.1 scene played with every reply under `**Grimoire:**` gives its NPCs only the player's posts: every reply is narration.
- **A missing source.** A bound card, lorebook or scenario that is gone (deleted while the app was closed) is reported as `("broken_source", <ref>)`, the exact ref the chat bound, alongside what follows from it (the character's `unnamed_actor` and `no_voice`). The turns are kept as they were, the chat still opens and takes posts, and nothing similarly named is bound in its place: a character without its card is silent (`nothing_to_generate`), a chat without its scenario has no director text (`no_scenario`).
- **The director reply's scenario text** is the scenario's premise, setting and instructions, or the chat's premise.
