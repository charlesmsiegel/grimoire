# Campaign tuning

Campaign-level structured controls can be placed into persistent world/campaign context and consumed by specific decision policies and generation tasks.

| Slider | Range | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Difficulty | Casual → Brutal | Challenge demands and required preparation |
| Drama–Simulation | Drama → Simulation | Dramatic selection versus autonomous world dynamics |
| Pacing | Slow Burn → Fast-Paced | Event frequency and scene transitions |
| Tone | Lighthearted → Grim | Emotional atmosphere |
| Lethality | Forgiving → Deadly | Probability and severity of lasting harm |
| Plot Pressure | Sandbox → Directed | External demands, deadlines, narrative pressure |
| Scarcity | Abundant → Scarce | Resource availability |
| Spotlight | Ensemble → Protagonist | Distribution of attention |

**Adaptive Difficulty** is a checkbox, not another slider. If enabled, adjust future obstacles and resource/strategic circumstances in response to player performance, targeting the selected difficulty. Do not secretly rewrite dice results or established facts. Make optional presets possible without losing independent controls.

Difficulty and lethality must be independent: an intellectually demanding or political game need not threaten death. These controls shape fictional challenges and presentation upstream of resolution; the game system's actual mechanics retain their integrity. A casual PbtA game still uses PbtA move procedures.

Open design questions for later specs: schema/defaults; overrides and migration; per-component consumers; calibration across rulesets; mid-campaign edits; transparency of adaptation; robust evaluations showing meaningful different play styles.
