# Obstacles, Encounters, Exploration and Player Agency

**Priority:** after a minimal resolution/consequence contract; world/location features can supply optional additional context.

## Goal

Give players **interesting problems to solve**, with meaningful information, choices, risk, preparation and consequences. An obstacle is a situation, not the same thing as a difficulty check; encounters organize opportunities to confront it.

## Work to plan

- **Obstacles:** generate or author challenges with coherent goal, actors, constraints, clues, resources, stakes, alternatives, escalations and failure modes. Obstacles need not be fights or even require formal mechanics.
- **Encounter Design:** place the challenge in an actual scene/world context, account for NPC agendas and available information, avoid surprise arbitrary retcons or dramatic fiat.
- **Quests / Scenarios:** model optional goals, dependencies, deadlines, commitments, branching outcomes and incomplete or abandoned objectives without forcing one linear plot.
- **Exploration / Travel:** surface locations, routes, hazards, encounters and meaningful changes in position/time/resources. Use map graph only when available; don't require coordinate-based worldbuilding.
- **Information Discovery:** preserve epistemic access, clue provenance, credible NPC knowledge, uncertainty and player deduction. A GM/narrator may know a secret without all NPCs knowing it.
- **Player Agency:** honor unconventional solutions, negotiation, stealth, clever preparation and refusal; resolution adjudicates intent under the chosen rules and facts, rather than steering the player to a prewritten outcome.
- **Consequences:** connect success, partial success, failure and alternate solutions to durable time, status, relationships, faction reactions, resource or story effects. Narration cannot rewrite the resolved result.

## Proving scenarios

A player must enter a guarded archive. The scene exposes several possible approaches—bribery, investigation, disguise, violence or abandoning the goal. The chosen world's current people, clues and hazards determine opportunity; mechanics if selected govern attempts. A failure still changes the situation and relationships rather than merely asking the player to roll again.

## Dependencies and boundaries

[Mechanics](mechanics.md) handles actual rules; [State Transactions](state-transactions.md) commits effects; [Narrative Design](storytelling.md) handles dramatic emphasis and long arcs; [Spatial World](spatial-world.md) and [World Simulation](world-simulation.md) provide optional setting, travel and time. [Campaign Tuning](campaign-tuning.md) may alter how many obstacles arise, not secretly change dice.

## Open questions

- What is the minimum persistent obstacle/quest representation usable in freeform play and across very different rulesets?
- Which stakes or constraints must be authored versus safely proposed by a model?
- How should partial success, hidden information, multiple parallel solutions and time deadlines be represented without a giant universal quest engine?
- What signals determine when an encounter is genuinely appropriate rather than arbitrary filler?
- How does player-agency evaluation detect railroading or outcome invention despite passing technical validation?
