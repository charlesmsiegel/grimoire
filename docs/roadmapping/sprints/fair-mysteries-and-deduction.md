# Fair Mysteries, Evidence and Player Deduction

**Outcome:** Provide solvable mysteries with persistent causes, discoverable evidence and the freedom to investigate along unforeseen routes.

## Scope and ownership
- Define an answerable hidden situation with motives, timelines, contradictions, clue provenance, red herrings and evidence access without making a predetermined investigation route mandatory.
- Allow players to ask unexpected questions, conduct tests, discover overlooked traces and infer solutions from established facts.
- Keep the hidden answer and already-established evidence stable when the player guesses, misses clues or proposes an unexpectedly clever explanation.
- Preserve fair uncertainty: some mysteries may be intentionally unresolved or have several compatible explanations, but that must be declared rather than covertly moving the solution.

## Milestones
1. Author a small locked-room or political mystery with a fixed answer and three independent approaches to key evidence.
2. Resolve player-directed investigations and make deductions matter without moving the culprit or fabricating post-hoc clues.
3. Support long-horizon investigative campaigns with competing hypotheses, deceptive witnesses and consistent forensic constraints.

## Moonshot
Players genuinely solve rich crimes, conspiracies and supernatural puzzles through reason, observation and creativity rather than guessing the narrator's planned next clue.

## Proving scenario
The PC investigates a suspect who was not the intended lead, reconstructs the timeline and identifies the culprit; previously established witness statements and evidence remain unchanged.

## Interfaces and independence
Owns **mystery fairness and evidence design**, not general obstacles ([Obstacles](obstacles-and-agency.md)) or the semantics of who knows what ([Knowledge](knowledge-and-perspective.md)). An authored small mystery can start with current world records.

## Open questions
- Which clues must be available through multiple routes?
- How are genuine ambiguity and multiple possible solutions handled?
- How can a mystery be evaluated for fairness without revealing it to players?
