# NPC Theory of Mind and Strategic Social Reasoning

**Outcome:** Characters can reason about what others believe, fear, suspect and intend without sharing the director's omniscient knowledge.

## Scope and ownership
- Represent selective first- and second-order beliefs as bounded, evidence-attributed hypotheses, not a full nested logical universe.
- Use these hypotheses to inform deception, persuasion, bluffing, suspicion, negotiation and emotional misreadings.
- Respect the distinction between a character's theory and verified story facts, with confidence, mistakes and later revisions.
- Evaluate both social plausibility and cost: deeper reasoning only for salient decisions.

## Milestones
1. Have one NPC draw a mistaken inference about another's knowledge while preserving the actual truth.
2. Support bluff/counterbluff and credible belief revision through an explicit action-selection test.
3. Expand to intrigue across factions with bounded nested beliefs and selective recall.

## Moonshot
An ensemble can engage in genuinely strategic social reasoning, including false assumptions about other people's assumptions, without perfect prediction or omniscience.

## Proving scenario
A spy falsely believes the mayor thinks the PC knows a secret, so the spy changes tactics; the director and underlying knowledge records remain correct.

## Interfaces and independence
Reads the [Knowledge and Perspective](knowledge-and-perspective.md) contract; [NPC Goals](npc-goals-and-autonomy.md) may consume beliefs but is not required to construct first-order hypothesis tests. Owns belief reasoning, not rumor transport.

## Open questions
- What depth of nested belief is worth the compute?
- How do we distinguish explicit evidence from actor conjecture?
- How should failed inferences be exposed to the player, if at all?
