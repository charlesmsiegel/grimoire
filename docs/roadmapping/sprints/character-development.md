# Characters, Progression and Party Management

**Priority:** after small individualized-actor and inventory foundations; ruleset-specific progression may arrive with the mechanics sprint.

## Goal

Long-running characters and companions **change meaningfully over time** while retaining identity, continuity, portrayal and mechanical integrity. Support PC/NPC advancement, nonhuman companions and varying party structures without treating every character as a player-controlled persona.

## Work to plan

- Represent persistent **character-specific progression** independently from reusable templates: skills, traits, injuries, training, experience, reputation, relationships, personal equipment, achievements and long-term change.
- Select advancement rules from the active mechanics module when one is present, including D&D advancement, WoD experience and supernatural growth, Ars Magica multi-season study, and PbtA playbooks. Mechanics-free development remains a fictional state update, not a fake character-sheet check.
- Support campaign-local versions, portrayal/art changes, character forks, resets and source defaults without overwriting old campaign history or changing world originals.
- **Party Management:** distinguish the controlled PCs, accompanying cast, individual companions, reserve/box members, absent allies and communal resources. Who can act or appear in a scene must not be inferred solely from possession or ownership.
- Monster-catching workflows: unique acquired individuals, trainer association, multiple captured members of the same species, release/trade, optional evolution and safe migration of moves/stat baselines. Keep capture devices and resources in Inventory.
- Manage party changes as explicit effects with readable provenance, time and scene consequences, not as mutable speaker lists.
- Support troupe-style and multi-protagonist structures as an extension; the UI for multi-PC turn batching and synchronized collaboration is a distinct candidate.

## Proving scenarios

Two separately captured monsters of one species develop different moves and relationships. One evolves without losing its personal ID or owner; a non-active monster remains available without being staged in the current scene. A WoD character advances an Attribute using appropriate costs while a mechanics-free NPC changes allegiance without a numeric stat sheet.

## Open questions

- Where should persistent **party/box/ownership** references live, and how do they differ from encounter cast or voice/speaker availability?
- Which advancement data is canonical campaign state versus a calculated view of a mechanics sheet?
- How do source creature/species changes, mechanical rebasing and personal overrides compose during evolution or respec?
- What permissions distinguish player control of a PC, a companion's agency, and an NPC's independent decisions?
- How much character change can be proposed automatically versus requiring explicit review?
