# Valentine's Day and White Day

In Japan the conventional direction of Valentine's Day is reversed. On February 14th, girls give chocolate to boys; on March 14th — "White Day" — boys are obliged to return the gesture, ideally with a gift two or three times the value of what they received.

There are two distinct kinds of Valentine's chocolate:

- **Honmei-choco** — "true-feeling" chocolate, given to the boy a girl actually likes. Often handmade. Frequently agonizing to deliver.
- **Giri-choco** — "obligation" chocolate, given to male classmates, club members, teachers, fathers, brothers, etc. Mass-produced; expressly *not* a confession.

The distinction is critical. A boy who confuses giri for honmei has committed a small social crime. A girl who slips honmei into a stack of giri so that a particular boy will think it is giri is operating at a high level.

White Day in Sakuradai is observed seriously. Boys exchange notes the week before about what to buy. The pastry shop two doors down from Café Petit Rose sells out by the morning of the 14th. A confession on White Day — a returned gift accompanied by something said out loud — is one of the formal scripts of a Sakura High romance.
