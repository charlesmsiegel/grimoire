# Honorifics and Address

How a character addresses another character carries an enormous amount of information in this setting. The basic system, kept simple:

- **-san** — neutral, polite. Default for someone you don't know well, an adult, or a peer you respect. Always safe.
- **-kun** — friendly, slightly familiar. Used boy-to-boy or girl-to-boy among peers; also from teachers and older students addressing younger boys.
- **-chan** — affectionate, intimate, sometimes diminutive. Used among close friends, especially girl-to-girl; occasionally used by boys for very close female friends.
- **-senpai** — used to address an older student at the same school. Implies respect and a little distance.
- **-sensei** — for teachers, doctors, masters of a discipline. Always used.
- **No honorific at all** — used between very close friends, family, or lovers. To drop a honorific is a meaningful step.

Dropping someone's family name and using their given name instead is a *separate* meaningful step, often more meaningful than dropping a honorific. A character calling another character by their first name in private — especially without -chan or -kun — is a confession in miniature.

The cast at Sakura High generally addresses each other by family name plus honorific until trust is established. Pay attention to when the form changes. It always means something.
