# Sakuradai Bridge

The pedestrian bridge across the river between the school district and the station district. Built in the seventies, painted green, never quite finished being painted. Wide enough for a half-meter pedestrian path on the east side, where the lampposts stand every twenty meters and glow through dusk in a soft sodium yellow.

The river below is shallow and slow and brown most of the year; in August it carries the lanterns of the summer festival. From the small viewing platform halfway across, on a clear night, you can see all the way to the Sakuradai Station signal lights and beyond them to the orange smear of the Tokyo skyline.

This is, by long Sakuradai tradition, the bridge where people say the difficult thing on the walk home — confess, break up, finally tell a friend something they should have told them years ago. The lampposts have witnessed an extraordinary amount of teenage truth.
