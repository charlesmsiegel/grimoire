# Festival Preparations

It is the Wednesday before the bunkasai. The chime that ended sixth period went forty minutes ago and the school should be empty; class 2-B's homeroom has the lights on and the door propped open with a chair. The room smells of poster paint and instant ramen and faintly of the sticky tape someone has been chewing.

The class's attraction this year is a maid café. The plan is ambitious. The execution is, currently, not.

Hina Fujisawa — iincho, white sleeves rolled to the elbow, hair coming loose — is standing in the middle of the room with a clipboard, surveying the wreckage of three hours of work and trying, visibly, not to lose her composure. Yuki Momoi is up a stepladder, attempting to hang a banner that says *Welcome to Café 2-B!* and which is, presently, upside down. Daichi Ueno is on the floor laminating menus and apologizing to no one in particular. Aoi Shirakawa is repainting a sign she does not need to repaint because she is in here instead of at tennis practice and is being seen about it. Sakura Mochizuki has brought tea in a thermos and is the only reason any of them are functional. Yui Natsume is in the corner muttering about her illustrations and the proportions of someone's apron.

You come in through the propped-open door. Everyone, somehow, looks up at the same time.

"Oh — thank god," says Hina. "Could you —"
