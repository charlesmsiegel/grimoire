# The Cherry Tree Legend

The cherry tree in the front courtyard of Sakura High is somewhere between sixty and ninety years old; the exact figure has been the subject of three different commemorative plaques, all giving different dates. What is not in dispute is the school superstition:

> A confession of love made beneath the tree on the day the first blossom opens cannot be refused.

The origin of the superstition is murky. The earliest reference appears in the 1968 yearbook, in a small unsigned essay; by the seventies it had passed into local lore. The local newspaper has photographed the tree every spring since 1972. Every year the bench beneath the tree is occupied at dawn on the morning the first blossom opens, sometimes by a hopeful student, sometimes by two friends pretending not to be hoping the same thing, sometimes by a third-year who has spent three years preparing.

Statistically, the superstition is not borne out. Anecdotally — depending on how you ask the question — it is. The yearbook keeps a running, informal tally.

Most students do not actually confess beneath the tree. The threat of having to is the romance.
