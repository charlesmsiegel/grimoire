# The Second Button

A tradition specific to Japanese high schools with the male uniform known as the gakuran — the high-collared dark jacket Sakura's boys wear in winter, with two rows of brass buttons down the front.

On graduation day, a girl who has cared for a boy through the school year may ask him for his **second button** — the one closest to his heart. To give the button is to acknowledge the feeling. To refuse is, in the polite indirect way of these things, an answer. The first button is the one most easily replaced; the second is the one a boy must consciously remove.

By the end of graduation day, the more popular third-years are missing all of their buttons; the less popular keep theirs intact and walk home laughing about it. There is no truth to the rumor that the school provides spare buttons in advance — except in the sense that every third-year boy now buys a packet of spare buttons in February.

It is, like the cherry tree legend, half a joke and half a serious thing. The joke holds it open; the seriousness gives it weight.
