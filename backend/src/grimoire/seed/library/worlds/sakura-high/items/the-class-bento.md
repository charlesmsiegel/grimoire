# The Class Bento

Not a single object but a recurring one. The homemade lunch — rice, a piece of grilled fish or egg, two or three vegetables, a small reddened pickle — packed by a student or their parent and brought to school in a small lacquered box. Eaten at desks or on the rooftop or in the courtyard.

Made by a parent, it is comforting and ordinary. Made by a student for themselves, it is a sign of independence. Made by a student for *another* student is something else entirely — an act of attention that, in the careful grammar of Sakura High, lies somewhere between giri-choco and a confession.

To accept a homemade bento from another student is to acknowledge the gesture. To return one is to participate in it. To eat it together in front of the class is a public declaration. The class enforces this grammar mostly by gossip, and partially by careful watching.

Hina Fujisawa makes her own bento. Sakura Mochizuki makes one occasionally, for someone specific, very carefully. Yuki Momoi makes one, badly. Souta Kirishima does not bring lunch and will accept anyone else's.
