# FamiMart Sakuradai-mae

The convenience store at the south end of the shopping street, open twenty-four hours, lit like an interrogation room and full of every necessary thing in the world. The plastic bags. The hot oden in winter. The umbrella stand by the door, which has rescued more first dates than the school nurse has rescued sprained ankles.

Souta Kirishima works the late shift here twice a week. The manager is a tired man in his fifties who refers to Souta as "trouble" with what is, on inspection, affection. The store is small enough that you can see everyone in it; large enough that you can pretend not to.

In a romance, the konbini is where the unexpected meeting happens — at eleven at night, in a borrowed sweater, buying the wrong umbrella.
