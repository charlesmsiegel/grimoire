Judge 1

Theory: A library is a user-owned tree of durable world and campaign records, with a campaign inheriting its world's records until it diverges. The store owns identity, local serialization and reviewed state transitions, while prompts and disposable indexes are derived views of those records.

Instead of: Instead of making SQLite or a materialized campaign copy the authoritative database, files establish membership and an overlay resolves inherited identity; the compiled database is explicitly disposable.

Judge 3

Theory: The library is an editable filesystem of authoritative records, while a campaign is a copy-on-write interpretation of its world with explicit deletion and identity ledgers. Atomic publication protects individual records, and declared lock domains serialize related read-validate-write sequences without promising distributed transactions.

Instead of: A replicated relational database or complete snapshot per campaign: editable files and live inheritance are intentional, and local publication/exclusion carry narrower guarantees.
