Judge 1

Theory: An editable filesystem library is the source of truth: worlds provide reusable material, campaigns resolve local overlays, and scenes and ledgers retain the consequences of play. Atomic publications, cooperative locks and identity fences protect local state, while projections and caches remain replaceable interpretations of those files.

Instead of: A relational server database with distributed transactions is another coherent design; the code instead preserves ordinary user-editable files and resolves effective campaign state over them, accepting weaker cross-device coordination.

Judge 3

Theory: A library is a collection of editable records whose files are authoritative, with worlds supplying shared material and campaigns owning only their divergences and play history. Publication, overlay resolution, stable identities and review journals protect that model while caches remain derived. The consistency model is cooperative local coordination and whole-record replacement, rather than a global transaction across devices.

Instead of: A transactional database with exported Markdown would also fit the product, but the readers and writers operate directly on user-editable files, and atomic.py explicitly limits its guarantee to individual published versions.
